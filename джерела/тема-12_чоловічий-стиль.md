# тема-12 · чоловічий стиль (v1 · 05.09.2026 · перша збірка)

## 0. Як читати

Формат — той самий, що в темах 4/10/11: кожне правило несе **WHEN / FORCE / BREAKS / CHANGES**,
тір T1–T4 (T1 виміряна емпірика · T2 інженерні стандарти · T3 консенсус названих практиків ·
T4 естетичні/культурні системи), мітку `[sourced]` / `[convention]` / `[folkloric constant]`
і код провенансу джерела: `[✓]` відкрито в цій сесії, є дослівна цитата · `[abs]` лише
анотація · `[⚠]` дійшло через переказ (конспект, рецензія) · `[—]` названо, не відкрито.

Кожне правило марковане: **[спільне]** — переноситься з жіночої бази без змін;
**[відмінне]** — той самий механізм, інші межі або знак; **[тільки чоловіче]** — аналога нема.

Три обмеження цієї збірки, сказані наперед:
1. **У фіді проєкту чоловічих оферів нема** (усі позиції «ж-»). Жодне правило нижче не міряне
   на SKU. Кожне CHANGES — опис рішення в коді, яке зміниться, а не вимір; це позначено.
2. **Провенанс чесний і після прогону №1 (05.09.2026) — середній.** `[✓]` стоїть під: Simon
   Crompton (Permanent Style — «Bespoke suede jacket: Cifonelli» 2016; «Change anything but the
   shoulders» 2008; «A sliding scale of formality» 2008; «Fitting a bespoke jacket in de Le Cuona
   linen» 2022; «Dressing for complexion» 2019), Derek Guy (X: collar gap 2023; «how a suit should
   fit» 20.04.2023; манжет 2024), Alan Flusser Custom («Fit Bits: Shirt Cuffs and Sleeves»),
   Debrett's, Emily Post (тема-5), Gray et al. 2014 (PLoS ONE — відкрито). Alan Flusser,
   *Dressing the Man* — дві дослівні цитати прочитано **в хостованій копії тексту**, сторінка
   оригінального видання не встановлена: позначено `[✓ копія]`; решта Flusser — `[⚠]`
   (конспекти). Bernhard Roetzel — `[⚠]`. G. Bruce Boyer, Hardy Amies, Antongiavanni — `[—]`.
   Торгові гіди ритейлерів взуття — «ринкова мова», не доказ.
3. **Жодного українського чоловічого практика з опрацьованим рішенням не знайдено.** Розділ X
   фіксує це як прогалину, а не закриває її.

## I.2 НЕ переноситься

- **K-KOH-10** (одна відкрита зона: ноги / декольте / плечі). У чоловічого образу інша граматика
  відкритості: зони — шия (розстебнутий комір), передпліччя (підкочений рукав), щиколотка

- **K-JEW-01** (кольє ↔ горловина) — у чоловічому образі ланцюжок рідкий і реєстро-залежний;

# PART II — Tailored fit (English-language sources)

Where the practitioner layer of this theme is thickest, and the only place where a real
worked decision over a named garment exists.

> ### K-MEN-01. The shoulder is the gate; the collar gap is its first symptom. [відмінне]
> Derek Guy (Die, Workwear!), X `[✓]`: the jacket collar "should always stay glued to your neck,
> even as you move" — a gap between collar and neck is the primary fault; shoulders "should
> terminate close to your shoulder joints". Verified on the primary in run 1: Guy's thread
> "how a suit should fit" (20.04.2023) lists the collar gap as item #1, and his 30.12.2023 post
> names it — "the distance between the jacket's collar and Kimmel's neck is known as a collar
> gap. This is bad." `[✓]`. Simon Crompton, Permanent Style, "Change anything but the shoulders"
> (2008) `[✓]`: "your shoulder should not be visible pushing at the cloth of the sleeve" — the
> named corroboration that the seam sits at the shoulder's edge and that this is the one thing a
> tailor cannot fix. Flusser, *Dressing the Man* `[⚠]`: the shoulder "should naturally follow the
> lines of the wearer's own shoulders" (Flusser's own "collar gap" wording not opened). The
> women's base already holds the same gate for outerwear as K-OUT-41 (`strong`, deliberately not
> `hard`).
> **FORCE:** `strong`, not `hard` — for the reason K-OUT-41 gives: RTW cannot alter the shoulder,
> so a hard gate empties the pool; a strong penalty ahead of every chest/waist term is the
> executable form. T3 `[sourced]` for the mechanism (two named practitioners, one stated
> symptom); any tolerance in centimetres is `[folkloric constant]` — the base's women's
> `fit.плече_допуск = 1.5 cm` is a *free-zone* tolerance and theme-3 §23 says a structural
> shoulder should be ~3× tighter; ~0.5 cm is the working default, uncalibrated.
> **WHEN:** any set-in, structured shoulder — suit jacket, sport coat, blazer, overcoat, structured
> overshirt. Register field = tailored / business / classic.
> **BREAKS:** raglan and knit shoulders (no seam to read); constructive dropped shoulder in the
> streetwear/oversized register (K-MEN-07 marker a) — the seam past the joint is the design,
> and the gate must read the register field first or it rejects the whole register. Posture:
> a stooped back or prominent shoulder blades can leave a residual collar gap on a jacket whose
> shoulder is right — the gap is then a posture fact, not a garment fault (render channel must
> not over-read it). Second worked decision, Crompton 2022 `[✓]`, bespoke linen jacket with
> wrinkles under the back armhole: "You could raise up that side of the jacket. Or you could
> pull it horizontally, cleaning the excess into the side seam." — mechanism: the arm's
> pressure on the armhole; three repairs, all bespoke-only. For RTW this is a verdict.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення, на фіді не міряно): *before* — jacket candidates are ranked by chest
> and waist ease. *after* — shoulder-seam position (from the person's shoulder width vs the
> garment's, or from the collar gap in the render) is scored first; a mismatch outranks a
> perfect chest. Code site (пропозиція): `fit.плече`, register-aware branch.

> ### K-MEN-02. Jacket length is set from the shoulder, and it moves three things at once. [тільки чоловіче] — a worked decision
> Simon Crompton, Permanent Style, on his bespoke Cifonelli suede jacket `[✓]`, verbatim:
> "we went slightly too far, and we will add about an inch from the shoulders. Adding that
> length from the shoulder will have the added benefit of moving the waist button down by the
> same amount and lengthening the lapel… The shoulder was meant to be very natural, but
> currently has a little touch of rope. Lorenzo is softening that too." Five years later, on the
> same garment: "It was too sharp and structured for a casual suede jacket, plus a bit short,
> a bit tight, and with bellied lapels that made it look too much like a tailoring jacket."
> This is the theme's only before/after by a named practitioner over a named garment, with the
> mechanism stated by the practitioner himself — the genre theme-4 Part XVI records as missing.
> **FORCE:** `strong` for the mechanism (length, button point and lapel length are one lever);
> `hint` for the number ("about an inch" — `[folkloric constant]`). T3 `[sourced]`.
> **WHEN:** bespoke / made-to-measure, or any garment with inlay in the shoulder seam. For RTW the
> rule is a *diagnostic*: a short jacket whose button sits high and whose lapel is short is one
> fault, not three, and by K-OUT-42 (take away, never add) it is a verdict, not a repair.
> **BREAKS:** garments meant to be short — bomber, harrington, cropped-register jackets — where
> the high button and short lapel are the design. Second break, from the same source: a casual
> jacket built with tailoring structure (rope, belly) reads as a suit jacket in the wrong
> register — structure must match the register, not only the fit.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — length, button stance and lapel length are three
> independent fields. *after* — one field with three outputs; the composer explains a short
> jacket once, and the register field decides whether "short" is a fault at all.

> ### K-MEN-03. Jacket length: the ratio, not the centimetre. [спільне механізмом, чоловіче числом]
> Derek Guy, X `[✓]`: "A suit jacket or sport coat should roughly bisect you halfway from the
> back of your collar to the floor when you're wearing heeled shoes. This gives the most
> flattering proportions." In his 20.04.2023 thread `[✓]` Guy states both rules and ranks them:
> "the jacket should always cover your bum. A better rule of thumb is that the jacket should
> bisect you halfway between your collar and the floor." Flusser, *Dressing the Man*
> `[✓ копія]` resolves the short-torso case himself: "the coat's length must remain as short as
> possible and yet cover the seat of his pants" — leg line first, seat as the floor. **The
> conflict recorded in v1 is closed:** two named practitioners from different lineages hold
> both rules, and the seat rule is the lower bound, the bisection the target. This is the
> men's instance of K-LEN-01 (length points scale with stature) and of K-OUT-02.
> **FORCE:** `default`. T3 `[sourced]` — two voices; the bisection ratio itself is `[convention]`
> (convergent, no measurement).
> **WHEN:** tailored jackets; standing; leather-soled shoes (a sneaker sole lowers the floor
> reference by ~2–3 cm and shortens the read).
> **BREAKS:** cropped registers; very tall persons, for whom the bisected length exceeds RTW
> lengths; short torso — the bisection target falls above the seat, and the seat rule wins as
> the floor (Flusser `[✓ копія]`); Crompton `[✓]` adds that a slanted hem can shorten the read
> without uncovering the seat.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — jacket length is absent from ranking. *after* — target
> length = ½ × (collar-to-floor from the person's height and neck point); deviation enters as a
> continuous penalty, never a filter. Needs the person's height (already a K-IO-02 input).

> ### K-MEN-04. Sleeve ends at the wrist bone; the shirt cuff shows a finger's width. [тільки чоловіче]
> Derek Guy, X `[✓]`: "you also generally want 1/4th to 3/8th of an inch of shirt cuff to peek
> out from your jacket sleeve (not a hard rule, but a good one)". Flusser `[⚠]`: at least ½ inch.
> Bernhard Roetzel, *The Gentleman* `[⚠]`: about 2/5 inch (≈1 cm). The base carried this as the
> folkloric constant "рукав: 0.5–1.5 см сорочки з-під манжета" (theme-3 §23). Three named
> practitioners now bracket it at 0.6–1.3 cm; a fourth voice (Articles of Style `[⚠]`) scales
> it with arm length — more cuff for short arms, less for long — which is the mechanism: the
> visible cuff is a proportion of the arm, not a constant.
> **FORCE:** `default`. T3. The window is still a `[folkloric constant]` — narrower, not verified.
> **WHEN:** jacket over a long-sleeve shirt, sleeves down, arms at rest.
> **BREAKS:** a watch under the cuff; casual jacket over a tee (no cuff — rule silent); rolled or
> pushed sleeves (a K-CRA-05 layout command, deliberately outside the rule).
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — sleeve length unscored. *after* — zero visible cuff and
> >2 cm visible cuff are both penalised; the 0.6–1.3 cm window is the default, marked folkloric
> in `outfit.REGISTRY`.

> ### K-MEN-05. Trouser break is a formality-and-date lever; hem width is set by the shoe. [тільки чоловіче]
> Four states, in descending fabric-over-shoe: **full · half · quarter (slight) · none**. Flusser
> `[⚠]`: the crease should bisect the kneecap and finish in the middle of the shoe; cuffed bottoms
> rest with a slight break. Derek Guy `[—]` (named, not opened here): a single break or the hem
> just grazing the shoe. The base carried "break: без залому = сучасно" as a folkloric constant;
> the practitioner layer confirms the *direction* (less break = current, more break = traditional)
> and adds the coupling to hem width: a slim hem takes quarter/none, a wide hem takes half/full;
> a wide hem with no break reads cropped, a slim hem with a full break reads puddled.
> **FORCE:** `default`. T3 `[convention]` for the states and the coupling. **"Less break =
> modern" is dated 2026** and carries an expiry field (theme-13 K-REG-04): it is a register
> reading, not a constant. Run 2 (05.09.2026) found the named, dated statement: Derek Guy, X,
> 27.08.2023 `[✓]`: "trousers should be hemmed with single or no break… don't let trousers pool
> around your ankles or be too short"; 12.09.2023 `[✓]`: with no break "you get a clean,
> uninterrupted line when standing still", while "a single or full break is a little more
> conservative" — and its mechanism: trousers ride up mid-stride, so more break means less sock
> shown when walking. Dated 2023, so the "current" reading is `default` with expiry, and the
> walking/standing trade-off goes into BREAKS. The coupling "slimmer hem → less break" has no
> quote — it is geometry (a narrower opening pools less), recorded as an engineering note, not
> as Guy's claim. Crompton 2008 `[✓]` adds: slant the hem to minimise break; turn-ups sit higher.
> **WHEN:** tailored trousers, hem visible, standing. Cuffed trousers require some break.
> **BREAKS:** wide-leg registers (2024–2026) legitimise full break over sneakers; cropped
> trousers with loafers are a declared citation (K-KOH-03), not a break error; no-break shows
> sock in motion (Guy) — for a person who walks a lot in the scenario (K-KOH-09 walking
> distance) a single break is the safer default.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — trouser length absolute. *after* — (hem width, break
> state) is a pair; mismatched pairs are penalised; the pair's "current" reading is looked up in
> theme-13 with its date.

> ### K-MEN-06. Shirt: the cuff is a gate, the collar is a proportion. [тільки чоловіче]
> Alan Flusser Custom, "Fit Bits: Shirt Cuffs and Sleeves" `[✓]`: "a shirt sleeve that simply
> hangs a bit longer than your jacket will ride up and disappear" — RTW cuffs are cut to fit any
> wrist and ride up when the arm moves; the repair is a sleeve slightly longer than the arm,
> blousing over a cuff cut trim to the wrist, so the cuff is held in place by circumference and
> the sleeve's extra length. The "hand slides through the fastened cuff = too large" test is
> Flusser's: run 2 found the sentence reproduced identically in three independent reader
> transcriptions of *Dressing the Man* — "if the hand can slide through the cuff opening
> without first unfastening it, the cuff's circumference is too large" — provenance stays
> `[⚠]` (no page-level primary), but the attribution is no longer in doubt. Collar:
> two-finger ease at the neck `[convention]`; collar spread scaled to face width and to the tie
> knot; collar points sit under the lapel `[convention]`; Flusser (notes) `[⚠]`: no part of the
> collar band should show above the knot.
> **FORCE:** `strong` for the cuff mechanism (named practitioner, mechanism stated, consequence
> measurable in the render: cuff disappears under the sleeve); `default` for collar ease; `hint`
> for spread ↔ face width — this is the same one-voice chain as K-OUT-14 (collar shape ↔ face
> shape, T4) and gets no more force than that.
> **WHEN:** shirt under a jacket, or shirt as the top layer with sleeves down.
> **BREAKS:** French cuffs with links (circumference set by the link); knit/polo collars (no spread
> variable); the two-finger ease is a `[folkloric constant]`.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — a shirt is sized by collar number alone. *after* — cuff
> circumference vs wrist and sleeve length vs wrist bone are gate inputs; collar spread is routed
> to tie-knot volume, not to taste.

> ### K-MEN-07. Deliberate oversize, men's version of K-FIT-06. [відмінне]
> Volume is intentional when three markers co-occur: (a) a *constructive* dropped shoulder (seam
> deliberately past the joint, sleeve head cut for it — not a too-big set-in shoulder);
> (b) one close-fitting element — a fitted base layer, a slim trouser hem or a bare ankle;
> (c) an exposed narrow point — wrist, ankle or neck. Without (b) and (c) volume is "too big",
> and K-MEN-01 applies. Vogue UA (Vogue MAN, 2026) `[✓]` reports the counter-move — fitted
> silhouettes returning in menswear — which is why the marker set, not the size, is the rule.
> **FORCE:** `strong` `[convention]` (inherited from K-FIT-06); the *current* balance point
> between oversize and fitted is dated and lives in theme-13.
> **WHEN:** register field = streetwear / oversized / relaxed; garment volume above the tailored
> ease band.
> **BREAKS:** tailored registers (volume is a fault there); outerwear, where volume is thermal
> (K-OUT-01 overrides proportion below the cold band).
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — a large jacket scores as ill-fitting regardless of
> register. *after* — with the register field set and the three markers present, the same jacket
> is not penalised; the composer's explanation names the markers, not the size.

# PART III — Proportion (English-language sources)

> ### K-MEN-08. Rise meets the button point; the "shirt triangle" is a proportion fault. [відмінне]
> K-FIT-04 transfers (rise is the primary leg/torso lever). The men's addition: with a jacket
> fastened, the waistband must sit at or above the jacket's button point, otherwise a triangle of
> shirt shows below the button and the leg line starts at the wrong height. Flusser `[⚠]`:
> trousers at the natural waist so that waistband and button point coincide.
> **FORCE:** `default`. T3 `[convention]`. Runs 1–2 searched Flusser/Boyer/Crompton/Guy for the
> "shirt triangle" and found nothing (○) — only forum language; Flusser's nearest text is the
> X-crease at the waist button (too tight), which is a different fault. The coupling stands on
> convention only.
> **WHEN:** jacket fastened; trousers with a visible waistband.
> **BREAKS:** open jackets (K-OUT-11 — opening is a repair of exactly this); low-rise registers
> (2000s revival) where the triangle is the citation.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — rise unscored. *after* — rise below the jacket button
> point is a default penalty; the composer prefers mid/high rise with jackets.

> ### K-MEN-09. Verticals and one-colour columns lengthen; nothing else in this class is stronger than `hint`. [спільне]
> K-SIL-04 and K-SIL-07 transfer as stated; the stripe literature is women's and mixed-gender,
> and nothing men-specific was found. Kept at `default` (verticals) / `hint` (stripes).

> ### K-MEN-11. Prefer over- to under-dressed — by at most one step. [спільне, з чоловічим джерелом]

> **BREAKS:** creative environments (K-KOH-07's half-step-under with one visible-quality piece);

> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — ±1 step off the target costs the same. *after* — −1 step

> ### K-MEN-13. Black suit is evening, formal or mourning; daytime business is navy or charcoal. [тільки чоловіче]

> **FORCE:** `hint` (lowered from `default` on 05.09.2026 — sila ≤ verification). T4
> `[convention]`.
> **WHEN:** daytime, band 5–8, register = business / classic.
> **BREAKS:** minimal and dramatic registers (black is their base colour); evening; a stated

> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — black scores as the most formal suit everywhere.

# PART V — Colour (English-language sources)

> ### K-MEN-15. Facial contrast sets the outfit's contrast; the menswear line says so independently. [спільне, другий голос]
> Flusser, *Dressing the Man* `[✓ копія]`: "If your hair is dark and your skin light, you have a
> contrast complexion." Two categories — contrast and muted — plus the instruction to repeat

> voice** to R-PC-03 (contrast level beats seasonal buckets, T2), and it agrees. Caveat from the
> same lineage — Crompton, Permanent Style, "Dressing for complexion" (2019) `[✓]`: the rule is
> "too vague to be of use to most men" — i.e. direction without a threshold.
> **FORCE:** `default`. T3 (Flusser) beside T2 (R-PC-03) — force stays with the measured rule;
> Crompton's caveat is why no number is attached.
> **WHEN:** near-face slots (shirt, tie, knit, jacket lapel); facial contrast measured in L*
> (R-PC-10 caveats on regional noise apply).
> **BREAKS:** dyed hair/beard and greying (K-TIME-01 — re-measure); black tie, where the code
> fixes high contrast regardless of face; low-contrast faces in a business band that demands a
> white shirt — resolve by lowering tie/jacket contrast, not the shirt.
> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — a men's profile defaults to navy + white for everyone.
> *after* — tonal navy/grey/brown sets rank up for low-contrast faces; stark navy/white for
> high-contrast; the same rule the women's base already runs, now with a men's anchor.
> Worked decision from the same author (run 3, Р-16): Alan Flusser Custom, Winter 2019 lookbook
> `[✓]` — for a client with "gold-blond autumnal coloring and blue eyes" the outfit is built as
> chestnut flannel + moss-green topcoat + russet tie, with the mechanism stated: "Once again the

# PART VIII — Body geometry (English-language sources)

> ### K-MEN-21. Men's shape labels route; zones and ratios decide. [спільне]

> **CHANGES** (опис рішення): *before* — nothing: no men's zone map exists. *after* — the women's

# ЧАСТИНА XIII — Наступний крок, який можна спростувати

**Тест.** Коли з'явиться перший чоловічий фід (будь-який український магазин з категорією
«піджаки/костюми»): взяти 20 карток піджаків і порахувати, скільки з них публікують ширину
плеча (або будь-який вимір, з якого її можна вивести).

**Прогноз, до прогону:** менше ніж 30 %.

**Якщо прогноз тримається:** K-MEN-01 — головні ворота цієї теми — не може спрацювати з даних
фіду і мусить іти через канал фото (K-CHN-01, канал рендера) або через вимір людини
(K-IO-02). Тоді код заводить K-MEN-01 як правило над рендером, а не над атрибутом.

**Якщо прогноз падає:** ширина плеча стає полем `param`, і K-MEN-01 заводиться як арифметика
з `fit.плече` у чоловічій гілці.
