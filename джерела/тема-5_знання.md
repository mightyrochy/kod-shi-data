# PART I — THE OCCASION AND DRESS-CODE SYSTEM

The only part of Theme 5 that a running code path consumes. `конвеєр.py` calls
`outfit.сценарій(…)`; `outfit.перевірка(…)` calls `формальність_образу(…)`.

**Source `[✓]`.** The Emily Post Institute, two pages, both fully read:
*Attire Guide: Dress Codes from Casual to White Tie*, and *Wedding Guest Attire: What to Wear*.
Contemporary, maintained, US-anchored, descended from Post's 1922 *Etiquette*.

**Status.** Codified convention (C). No effect sizes, and none claimed. This is the closest
thing Theme 5 has to a practitioner ground truth, and it is still convention, not measurement.

## 1.1 The ten codes, women's register, mapped to the 1–10 scale

The code contents are the source's; the scale mapping is mine.

| Code | Women's register | Band |
|---|---|---|
| **Casual** | sundress; skirt any length; good jeans or khakis; plain tee without slogans, polo, turtleneck; casual button-down. Clean, not house-baggy | 3–4 |
| **Dressy casual** | dress; skirt with dressy top; dressy trouser outfit; good jeans with dressy top | 4–6 |
| **Business casual** | skirt, khakis or trousers; open-collar shirt, knit top or sweater — no spaghetti straps, no décolletage; dress | 5–6 |
| **Semi-formal** | short afternoon or cocktail dress; the "little black dress"; long dressy skirt with top; dressy separates | 6–8 |
| **Festive** | cocktail dress; long dressy skirt and top; dressy trousers or separates; LBD — **with seasonal colour** | 6–8 |
| **Business formal** | suit; business-style dress; dress with a jacket; hosiery optional in summer; heels low or high | 7–8 |
| **Black tie optional** | floor-length gown, *or* cocktail dress, *or* LBD, *or* dressy separates | 7–9 |
| **Black tie** | floor-length evening gown; dressy cocktail dress; dressiest LBD | 8–9 |
| **Creative black tie** | as black tie, plus deliberately distinctive accessories | 8–9 |
| **White tie** | floor-length evening gown; long gloves optional | 10 |

Three mechanisms the source states outright:

- **Darker suit = more formal.** An explicit lightness→formality link — the named-source
  backing for `L_вікно=(0,45)` on evening formality, which rested only on corpus K-KOH-06.
- **After 6 p.m., err toward formality.** Stated inside the semi-formal entry; see §1.3 for the
  much stronger structural version.
- **Business codes defer to the employer.** The guide tells the reader to follow their company's
  code over the generic one. Workplace formality is not derivable — it is local authority.

## 1.2 The wedding matrix — a second, incompatible Post taxonomy

The wedding page uses **Formal / Semiformal / Informal**, each split by time of day:

| Tier | Daytime (women) | Evening (women) |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal** | cocktail or dressy afternoon dress | long evening dress, or dressy cocktail dress, depending on local custom; gloves optional |
| **Semiformal** | dressy afternoon dress, suit, or trouser suit | cocktail dress; dressy trouser suit |
| **Informal** | afternoon dress; dressy skirt or trousers with blouse | afternoon or cocktail dress |

**Finding — one source, two vocabularies that do not align.** The attire guide's "semi-formal"
(cocktail dress, LBD) is not the wedding guide's "Semiformal" (daytime: suit or trouser suit).
The wedding guide's "Informal" *evening* is a cocktail dress, which the attire guide files under
semi-formal. **The same word denotes different levels in different contexts inside one
authority.**

Consequence: an invitation string like "semi-formal" **cannot be mapped to one number.** The
system must resolve a dress-code string against the event type before assigning a band, or
carry the ambiguity forward as a range.

## 1.3 The evening lift is graduated by tier, not a flat +1

Reading the wedding matrix vertically gives the real structure of the day/evening rule:

| Tier | Day → evening move |
|---|---|
| Formal | cocktail/afternoon dress → **floor-length gown** — roughly +2 |
| Semiformal | suit or trouser suit → **cocktail dress** — roughly +1 |
| Informal | afternoon dress → afternoon **or** cocktail dress — roughly +0.5, optional |

The lift is **largest at the top and vanishes toward the bottom.** It is not a constant added
to every venue. This is much stronger evidence than the single "after 6" sentence and it
directly specifies the fix in Part VI: the bump belongs in the dressable band and should scale
with tier, not apply flat.

## 1.4 Practical vetoes the wedding page supplies

The system's veto layer has none of these:

- **Religious or cultural head covering may be required.**
- **Bare shoulders/arms, or open-toed shoes, may be inappropriate** depending on the service —
  a coverage veto, not a palette veto.
- **Local custom varies by region** in conservatism; the source names this as a live input.
- **The invitation and the ceremony time are the primary formality signal** — the source's own
  first instruction, and independent support for treating occasion as user-supplied (Part IV).

## 1.5 Three structural findings

**(a) For womenswear a garment's formality is a range, not a point.**
The cocktail dress appears in **five** codes plus three wedding cells. The LBD appears in four.
Dressy separates in three. The same object is correct from roughly 6 to 9 depending on
occasion, fabric and styling. The code stores `ТИП_ФОРМАЛЬНІСТЬ["коктейльна_сукня"] = 7`, a
point estimate suppressing a two-to-three step legitimate span.

**(b) Codes are bands by design, and the source says so structurally.**
"Black tie optional" and "creative black tie" exist precisely to hand the guest a choice within
a band. Named-source confirmation of K-KOH-05, which was corpus-internal until now.

**(c) The source under-specifies womenswear.**
Men's black tie: jacket, trouser match, shirt-front construction, studs, cuff links, bow-tie
material, cummerbund or vest, braces, glove prohibition, shoe finish, sock colour. Women's black
tie: three garment categories and the word "dressy". Every women's entry is category names plus
an intensifier; every men's entry is componentry. **The convention itself is thinner on the side
this product serves.** The same asymmetry appears in §2.4 from an unrelated source.

## 1.6 What `КОДИ_ДІАПАЗОНИ` is missing

Current keys: white_tie, black_tie, cocktail, business_formal, business_casual, smart_casual,
casual.

- **Absent and real:** dressy casual, black tie optional, creative black tie, festive, and the
  wedding tiers formal/semiformal/informal × day/evening. "Black tie optional" is the commonest
  invitation wording after black tie itself — today a real invitation string has nowhere to land.
- **Present but not codes:** `cocktail` is a *garment* the source places in four codes;
  `smart_casual` is European terminology Post does not use — its content is "dressy casual".
- **Festive is the only code with a positive colour instruction** — seasonal colour is invited.
  `НАГОДА["свято"]` currently says only that festive palettes are geo-variable.
- **"Afternoon dress" is a garment category the project does not model at all**, and it carries
  three of the six wedding cells.

# PART II — WHAT DRESS SIGNALS TO OTHERS

## 2.1 The frame `[✓]`

Hester N., Hehman E. (2023). *Dress is a Fundamental Component of Person Perception.*
Personality and Social Psychology Review 27(4), 414–433. DOI 10.1177/10888683231157961.

**Genre, stated first because it governs everything after.** A narrative review. No experiments,
no new data. Its tier does not transfer to the claims it carries.

Impression = target dress × target face/body × context, all read through the perceiver's lens —
cultural knowledge, stereotypes, attitudes, preferences. Four kinds of dress inference: social
category, cognitive state, status, aesthetics. The authors state these four were **not
empirically derived** — themes that emerged while sorting literature. A filing system, not a
finding.

Why dress separates from face/body: face and body are stable, trait-like, biologically
range-restricted, barely caused by identity. Dress is state-like, malleable, **chosen**, and
culturally unbounded. Because it is chosen it can signal identity better — but only to the
extent it was *meant* to. Dress worn without intent is residual, not signal.

Two worked examples that make it operational:
- Safety pins read as punk identity **only** to a perceiver holding that cultural knowledge.
  Without the knowledge the cue does not exist — not misread, absent.
- The same jeans produce different impressions depending on what they are worn with, who wears
  them, where, when, and who is looking.

**Operative rule.** No context-free "this outfit says X". The pipeline already takes occasion,
venue and hour as explicit inputs, so nothing needs building — one thing needs *not* building:
a general impression output that reports meaning without conditioning on audience.
R-PP-01 stays `hard`.

**The review's own generality limit:** findings rest predominantly on **White American
perceivers judging White American targets.** R-PP-09 (do not transplant the lens to the
Ukrainian market) is correctly derived.

## 2.2 Fit quality reads as economic status — on competence traits only `[✓]`

Howlett N., Pine K., Orakçıoğlu I., Fletcher B. (2013), *Journal of Fashion Marketing and
Management* 17(1), 38–48.

Online, 274 complete respondents. One male model in a bespoke suit and an off-the-peg suit,
**matched on colour (dark navy) and cloth (herringbone)**, differing only in minor fit details;
bespoke retailed at roughly three times the price. **Images were faceless** — head cropped,
isolating the garment. Exposure capped at five seconds, minimum three.

Bespoke scored higher on confidence, success, salary and flexibility — and **not on
trustworthiness**, which showed no gain. Hester & Hehman list the three positives and drop the
null. The boundary matters: fit quality buys competence-type impressions, not warmth-type ones.
A system promising "you will be trusted more" would overclaim.

**Moderator the review omits: higher-earning perceivers rated both suits lower.** Cue strength
depends on the viewer's own economic position — the perceiver lens made concrete, and a direct
argument against any universal looks-expensive score.

Scope: one male model, menswear tailoring, no face, UK online sample. The mechanism is a
*making* property rather than a price tag, which is why it could in principle transfer to
`посадка` — but it has never been tested on womenswear.

**Gap this opened `[—]`:** the same group published Howlett et al. 2015 (*Sex Roles* 72,
105–116) on provocativeness × occupational status, reporting negative perceptions especially of
**a woman in a senior position**; related work finds women's competence judgements differ by
trouser suit versus skirt suit. Neither is in Hester & Hehman. Both are womenswear. Not read.

## 2.3 Colour preference is not universal — and the mechanism behind colour-mood mappings inverts `[✓]`

Taylor C., Clifford A., Franklin A. (2013), *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*
142(4), 1015–1027. **Year is 2013, not the 2012 the review cites** (epub Nov 2012).

British adults versus Himba adults, a non-industrialised group in rural Namibia chosen because
they are largely outside global consumer culture.

**Direction, which the review's one line omits:** British preferred bluish hues and disliked
yellow-greens; **Himba showed low preference for bluish hues and high preference for saturated
red, orange, yellow, chartreuse and green.** Close to inverted on the blue axis. Himba showed
**none** of the supposedly universal sex differences.

**The WAVE result — the part that matters most here.** The weighted-affective-valence model
holds that colour preference tracks the average valence of objects associated with a colour; it
is the intuition under every colour-psychology and colour-mood instrument. British: r = .81,
66% of variance (men .86/74%, women .67/45%). Himba: 23% of variance and the correlation was
**significantly negative**, r = −.48; Himba men's object-valence correlation r = −.70. The
mechanism does not weaken across cultures — it **reverses**. Later industrialised samples
explain 44–73%, so it is unstable even at home.

**Consequence.** The standing decision to import Kobayashi's Color Image Scale for its
palette-signature mechanism while discarding its mood adjectives moves from precaution to
**sourced decision**: Kobayashi is a Japanese consumer-culture instrument, and the
object-association basis of colour affect is culture-bound and can invert. Any Ukrainian
colour-mood mapping must be measured locally, never imported. Note Sorokowski, Sorokowska &
Witzel (2014) argue the opposite for sex differences specifically — the literature is live.

## 2.4 The gender split in the status channel `[abs]`

Schmid Mast M., Hall J. A. (2004). *Who Is the Boss and Who Is Not? Accuracy of Judging Status.*
Journal of Nonverbal Behavior 28(3), 145–165.

48 university employees photographed interacting with a coworker; two perceiver samples;
behavioural and appearance cues independently coded.

Perceivers judged status in **women** from behavioural cues — downward head tilt, lowered
eyebrows. They judged status in **men** from appearance, specifically **formal dress**. In both
cases they were right to do so: formal dress tracked status for male targets and did not track
it for female targets.

The only study in this literature that split the dress→status channel by target gender, and it
found the channel present for men and absent for women. One study, N=48, one US workplace, 2004,
office formality, abstract-level reading only. It does not prove absence for women in general.
It proves the corpus has **no evidence of presence** for the people this system serves.

Convergence worth noting: a US etiquette institution (§1.5c) and a nonverbal-behaviour lab both
specify men's dress formality far more sharply than women's. That is a property of the evidence
base and should be stated whenever a formality number is quoted.

## 2.5 Status cue speed and involuntariness `[abs]`

Oh D., Shafir E., Todorov A. (2020). *Economic status cues from clothes affect perceived
competence from faces.* Nature Human Behaviour 4(3), 287–293.

Nine studies. Participants rated **competence of faces in frontal headshots**. The same face was
shown with upper-body clothing pre-rated by independent judges as looking "richer" or "poorer" —
though not notably perceived as such when described explicitly. The same face in richer clothes
was judged significantly more competent. The effect persisted at 129 ms exposure, under explicit
warning that the cue was uninformative, and under instruction to ignore it — in one study with
considerable incentive.

The authors frame this as an uncontrollable bias that **adds a hurdle for low-income people.**

**Licenses:** a coarse perceived-cost signal on the torso moves competence judgements fast and
involuntarily. **Does not license:** anything about composition, colour relations, silhouette,
fit, accessories or pairing. **Effect magnitudes not obtained** — so this can support "this
happens", never "weight it by *x*". It cannot enter a scoring function.

Two of these were spot-checked at source (§2.2, §2.3) and **both had lost something material.**
A 2-for-2 rate means the rest should be assumed damaged, not merely uncertain.

| Finding | Attributed to |

| Non-stereotypical clothing slows categorisation by activating competing categories | Freeman et al. 2011 |

The cue-validity row is the one to open first: a recommender that optimises perceived signal is
optimising *utilisation*, and must be able to flag the invalid-but-used case rather than
reinforce it. That is a design constraint resting entirely on a summary.

## 2.7 Harm `[✓]`

The review documents dress inferences activating stereotypes: Afrocentric hairstyles drawing
lower professionalism ratings for Black women; sexualised clothing drawing attributions of
immorality to women; LGBTQ+ people reporting pressure to dress "to" an identity, producing
documented tension between subcultural and individual authenticity.

Advice framed as "dress this way so you are read correctly" launders the reader's bias into the
wearer's wardrobe. Frame output as a **chosen** signal the person controls, never a norm they
must satisfy. R-PP-08 stays `hard`.

# PART III — COLOUR COORDINATION `[✓]`

Gray K., Schmitt P., Strohminger N., Kassam K. S. (2014). *The Science of Style: In Fashion,
Colors Should Match Only Moderately.* PLOS ONE 9(7): e102772.

**Design.** 239 mTurk participants (69% women, mean age 35.4). Four palettes — two women's, two
men's — of **four colours each**, sixteen colours in the whole study. Each participant saw 30
combinations drawn quasi-randomly from the 256 possible within one palette. Stimuli were flat
illustrated outfit graphics. No fabric, no texture, no body, no face.

**How coordination was measured — the decisive detail.** Not in any colour space. Participants
rated **every pair of colour swatches** within a palette on three Likert items — coordinated,
matching, similar (α > .81). Ratings were aggregated per outfit and **z-scored within palette**.
There is no CIELAB, no ΔE, no CIEDE2000, no hue angle, no chroma anywhere in the paper.

**Outcome.** "Fashionableness" — fashionable / good / liked (α > .95), also z-scored within
palette. Not impressions of a person.

**Results.** Women's: linear R² = .18, F(1,58) = 13.04, p = .001, qualified by quadratic
R² = .44, F(2,57) = 22.23, p < .001 — the curve carries about twice the variance of the trend.
Men's: linear not significant; quadratic R² = .28, F(2,57) = 11.18, p < .001.

**Why no threshold transfers.** Predictor and outcome are both z-scored *within palette*. The
peak sits at a **relative position inside whatever range the experimenters sampled** from a
four-colour set. Change palette, sampling, or move to a real feed and no fixed coordination
value holds. The paper establishes a *shape*, no *point*. The authors concede moderate matching
is not the only key to fashion and leave naturalistic validity to future work.

**Construct drift downstream.** Gray et al. measured how fashionable an outfit *graphic* looks.
Hester & Hehman render it in one hedged sentence as the most positive **first impressions** — of
a person. Different dependent variable. The corpus inherited the drifted version and attached a
person-perception rule ID to it.

**What survives.** A testable hypothesis: outfit colour coordination may relate to appeal as an
inverted U, possibly with stronger curvature for womenswear. Measurable on the Ukrainian feed
with the project's own metric. Not a rule; yields no constant.

**Failure conditions to carry with it.** Sixteen colours; flat graphics; no person; US mTurk
2014; "fashionable" as outcome; within-palette normalisation. Any one can flip the sign.

# PART IV — OCCASION CANNOT BE INFERRED FROM THE GARMENT `[abs]`

Becattini F., De Divitiis L., Baecchi C., Del Bimbo A. (2023). *Fashion recommendation based on
style and social events.* Multimedia Tools and Applications 82, 38217–38232.
DOI 10.1007/s11042-023-15290-4; preprint arXiv:2208.00725.

**Architecture (verified from the open preprint).** A base recommender plus two **independent
post-hoc classifiers** — colour-mood and social event — that filter its proposals. The
classifiers attach to any recommender: a modular gate, not part of the generator. This is the ML
form of the project's compositor-plus-critic pattern — independent confirmation the pattern
works, generating no new rule.

**Fashion4Events.** Roughly 400k garment images labelled with social event, built by segmenting
garments out of Flickr event photographs (the USED dataset). Openly released.

**The property that governs use.** Ground truth is *what people wore in photographs taken at
that event* — guests, staff, performers and passers-by mixed. **Descriptive co-occurrence, not a
normative dress code.** The K-KOH layer answers the other question: what is appropriate. Using
one as the other is a category error; using it as a weak, explicitly-labelled prior over a
normative layer is legitimate.

**Not verified.** The corpus's accuracy figures — 78.6% style, 49.9% event on 14 classes, the
label-entropy numbers — are behind the MTAP paywall. Carried over, unverified.

**Decision, independent of those numbers.** Occasion stays a **user-supplied input**. It follows
from the labelling scheme alone: even a perfect classifier trained on Flickr event photos learns
what was worn there, not what suits there. The reverse direction — garment → events it suits —
is at most a soft versatility hint, never a gate. Emily Post independently agrees (§1.4): the
invitation and the ceremony time are the primary signal.

**One transferable method.** Deterministic rule-based auto-labelling → purity threshold
discarding ambiguous cases → human validation on the remainder. A cheap golden-set recipe for
colour family or formality band.

# PART V — FASHION AS A SYSTEM: THE TWO CLASSIC SOURCES `[✓]`

Both are English-language primary sources in the public domain. Rendered here in the original
English. Read for what they change in the pipeline, not for completeness as sociology.

## 5.1 Simmel — imitation and demarcation

Simmel G. (1904), *Fashion*, International Quarterly 10, 130–155; reprinted American Journal of
Sociology 62(6), 541–558 (1957). German versions of 1895, 1905 and 1911 differ from one another,
so "Simmel says" is always version-dependent.

**The core statement.** Fashion is a form of imitation and so of social equalization, but,
changing incessantly, it differentiates one time from another and one social stratum from
another. It unites those of a social class and segregates them from others.

**The mechanism, in Simmel's own terms.** The latest fashion affects only the upper classes. As
soon as the lower classes begin to copy that style — crossing the line of demarcation the upper
classes have drawn and destroying the uniformity of their coherence — the upper classes turn
away from it and adopt a new one, which differentiates them again. The lower classes strive
upward and meet least resistance in exactly those fields governed by fashion, because there
mere external imitation is most readily applied.

Three constraints Simmel attaches, all of which limit how far the mechanism generalises:
- The process **quickens as wealth increases.**
- Fashion **does not exist in tribal and classless societies.**
- It operates on **externals and superficialities, where irrationality does no harm** — which is
  precisely why it can move so fast.

**The one operationally useful claim.** Some forms are intrinsically more suited to the
modifications of fashion than others: **the internal unity of the forms called "classic" makes
them immune to change.** This gives actual content to the "timeless" objective in R-SYN-02,
which until now was a word. It is also **testable on a feed over time**: if internal unity of
form is what resists fashion, garments whose proportions are self-consistent and carry no
arbitrary detail should show longer catalogue lifetimes than trend-carrying equivalents. That
is a measurement the project can run once it has feed snapshots across seasons.

**The standing counterweight `[—]`.** Blumer (1969) replied that fashion is less a top-down
divisive device than an expression of **collective taste selection** — buyers converging, not
elites dictating. Not read; recorded so the Simmel mechanism is not carried as settled.

## 5.2 Veblen — dress as pecuniary evidence

Veblen T. (1899), *The Theory of the Leisure Class*, ch. 7, "Dress as an Expression of the
Pecuniary Culture", pp. 167–187.

**Why dress.** Veblen picks dress as the aptest illustration of his whole apparatus: no line of
consumption illustrates conspicuous waste better, because dress is permanently on display.
Expenditure on dress is undertaken for show more than for protection — people will accept
discomfort from the weather rather than appear unfashionable.

**The mechanism, which is sharper than "expensive things signal wealth".** Where wasteful
expenditure and the show of abstention from effort are carried to the point of **obvious
discomfort or voluntarily induced physical disability**, the inference is that the wearer does
not perform this display for her own pecuniary repute but on behalf of someone to whom she
stands in economic dependence. His concrete list: the high heel, the skirt, the impracticable
bonnet, the corset — the corset being, in his terms, substantially a mutilation that
permanently and visibly disables the wearer from work.

**Novelty is structural, not incidental.** Dress must be contemporary; it must conform to a
fashion that constantly changes. Conspicuous waste favours novelty, because a garment visibly
of last season is visibly cheap.

**The refinement dynamic — quiet luxury, stated in 1899.** Once the wealthy class grows large
enough, conspicuous waste becomes **more subtle**, and more obvious displays come to be regarded
as **vulgar**. Powdered wigs and gold lace fell out of use on exactly this logic. Discrimination
between styles then becomes ever finer, because members of higher classes care primarily about
the esteem of their peers, not of the general public.

**Genealogy correction.** The corpus records inconspicuous consumption as a 2015 finding
(Eckhardt et al., §2.6). The mechanism is in the 1899 primary source, in the same chapter the
corpus already cites. Eckhardt is a modern empirical test of a Veblen claim, not an independent
discovery — and the corpus was counting them as two voices.

**The claim about stable costume — content for "timeless".** Veblen argues that costumes which
bear the test of time and perspective were worked out by populations poorer, more homogeneous,
stable and immobile than modern urban ones — places where pecuniary emulation ran through
conspicuous *leisure* rather than conspicuous *consumption of goods*. Mobile wealthy urban
populations set the pace of fashion and produce unstable costume. This converges with Simmel's
"internal unity" claim from a completely different direction, and the two together are the only
substantive account of "timeless" the theme contains.

**Where Veblen collides with the evidence, and the evidence wins.** Veblen holds that propriety
requires **more** unremitting attention to expensive display in women's dress than men's, because
a patriarchal inheritance makes it the woman's function to put her household's ability to pay in
evidence. Mast & Hall 2004 (§2.4) measured the inference side of this and found the opposite:
perceivers did **not** use women's formal dress to judge status, and were right not to, because
it did not track status for female targets. Veblen describes an intent to display; the one study
that tested reception found the display is not read. **For a women's styling system, the reception
side is what matters, and decision 2 in Part VI follows from it.**

**Scope.** Both texts are turn-of-the-century, upper-class Western, and entirely theoretical —
no data, no measurement, no test set. Their force ceiling is `hint`: they are the explanation
reservoir the compositor draws on when it must say *why*, and they license no threshold.

**What they jointly establish that is architecturally real.** "Looks expensive", "on-trend" and
"timeless" are **three separate objectives driven by different mechanisms** — pecuniary evidence,
the imitation-flight cycle, and internal unity of form respectively — and they routinely pull
against each other. Veblen's own refinement dynamic is the clearest case: past a certain point,
looking expensive requires *not* looking expensive. The compositor must therefore carry an
explicit target rather than blending them, noting that decision 2 has removed the first from the
buildable set for this product.

# PART VII — CODE DECISIONS, WITH BEFORE/AFTER

**1. Delete the planned palette-ΔE coordination target.** R-PP-06 → `hint`, folkloric.
*Before:* near-monochrome emits a coordination remark. *After:* passes silently — correct, since
near-monochrome is an ordinary worn register with no measured basis for penalty.

**2. Do not build a status / looks-expensive axis.** No verification for the target user (§2.4);
the one usable mechanism is fit quality, tested only on menswear (§2.2); the perceiver-earnings
moderator means there is no universal scale anyway.
*Before:* a tailored blazer outfit outranks an equal-priced soft-knit outfit, explained as
"reads as higher status". *After:* both ranked on fit, colour and occasion range, and nothing
claims to know how the wearer will be socially graded.

**3. Make the evening lift graduated and band-gated.** Currently `if година >= 18: lo += 1;
hi += 1`, unconditional. The source is graduated: ~+2 at formal, ~+1 at semiformal, ~+0.5 and
optional at informal, and silent below.
*Before:* park at 19:00 → base (2,4) → target 3–5, flagging a correct tracksuit as
under-target; home at 20:00 → 2–3. *After:* apply only when `hi >= 5`, scaled by tier.

**4. Give garment types variable-width formality intervals and keep the tolerance.**
**Measured, Part VIII.** A uniform ±1 interval is a no-op — `розкид ≤ 2` on points is already
identical to it. What works is width drawn from how many Post codes list the garment
(cocktail dress 6–9, jeans 3–5, tracksuit 2–2) **combined with the existing tolerance of 2**:
removes 40.4% of K-KOH-02 firings, adds none.
*Before:* tee + sheath dress + trainers, and tee + jeans + heels, are flagged as formality
conflicts. *After:* both pass — correctly, since they are ordinary high-low looks. The point
model was systematically penalising the register K-CRA-03/K-KOH-03 endorses.

**5. Repair and extend `КОДИ_ДІАПАЗОНИ`.** Add dressy casual, black tie optional, creative black
tie, festive, and the wedding tiers as (tier × day/evening) pairs. Retire `cocktail` as a code
key — it is a garment. Map `smart_casual` onto dressy casual or drop it. Add "afternoon dress"
to the garment vocabulary.

**6. `ФОРМ_РОЗКИД_МАКС = 2` is aggressive, not toothless. Measured.** Over a uniform draw of the
type space it **rejects 74.1%**; median spread is 3, so the median random combination fails. With
decision 4 the rejection rate falls to 44.2%. It can keep `strong` on a measured pass rate,
which it could not before. Real-feed distribution is not uniform, so this is an upper bound on
strictness.

**7. Remove or relabel `сезон == "літо" → −1`.** No source supports a one-step summer reduction.
Invented constant; folkloric until calibrated.

**8. Add the wedding vetoes (§1.4):** head covering may be required; bare shoulders/arms and
open-toed shoes may be inappropriate by service type; regional conservatism as an input. These
are coverage vetoes on the item, not palette vetoes, and the veto layer has none of them.

**9. Do not map a dress-code string to a single number.** Post uses "semi-formal" at two
different levels in two contexts (§1.2). Resolve the string against event type, or carry the
range.

# PART VIII — RUN EXECUTED, RESULTS

Predictions were recorded before the run. `сценарій()` was executed over 12 scenarios; the
formality filter was measured over all 441 upper×lower×shoe triples of `ТИП_ФОРМАЛЬНІСТЬ`.
**Three of four predictions were wrong.**

**Prediction 1 — CONFIRMED, and worse than predicted.** The evening +1 fires in **4 of 12**
scenarios whose venue ceiling is below 5: park 19:00 (2,3)→3–4; gym 18:30 (1,2)→2–3; long
journey 21:00 (2,4)→3–5; **home 20:00 (1,2)→2–3**. Being at home at 8 p.m. demands more
formality than at 2 p.m.

**Prediction 2 — REFUTED, opposite direction.** Predicted >80% pass, therefore not a filter.
Measured **25.9% pass, 74.1% reject.** Spread distribution over 441 triples — 0: 0.9%, 1: 8.6%,
2: 16.3%, 3: 29.5%, 4: 21.8%, 5: 13.4%, 6: 7.5%, 7: 2.0%. Mean 3.43, median 3.

**Prediction 3 — REFUTED.** Predicted the `(3,6)` default spans over half the scale. Measured
**40%** — four levels of ten. The related figure that does hold: **16 of 24 garment types (67%)**
fall inside it, because the type table is dense in 3–6. Loose in item terms, tight in scale
terms — but not what I predicted.

**Prediction 4 — REFUTED, and the reason is the finding.** Predicted intervals remove >50% of
K-KOH-02 firings. With uniform ±1 intervals: **0% removed**, both criteria returning exactly 327
conflicts. The reason: |a−b| ≤ 2 is *exactly* the condition that [a−1,a+1] and [b−1,b+1] overlap.
**`ФОРМ_РОЗКИД_МАКС = 2` is already an interval model in disguise.** A second attempt with
variable widths but no tolerance was worse — 85.7% conflicts, flagging hoodie+leggings+trainers.

| Model | Conflicts / 441 | Removed vs point model | New false positives |
|---|---|---|---|
| point + spread ≤ 2 (current) | 327 (74.1%) | — | — |
| uniform ±1 intervals | 327 (74.1%) | 0% | 0 |
| Post-width intervals, no tolerance | 378 (85.7%) | 2.1% | 58 |
| **Post-width intervals + tolerance 2** | **195 (44.2%)** | **40.4%** | **0** |

Short of the predicted 50%, so refuted as stated. The removed cases are diagnostic: tee + sheath
dress + trainers, tee + jeans + court shoes, blazer + jeans + loafers — every one a standard
contemporary high-low look. **The substantive finding no prediction anticipated:** the
point-value model was systematically flagging high-low as a formality error, contradicting
K-CRA-03/K-KOH-03. Only a run could show it.

**What the run did not settle.** No real feed was parsed; the type space was drawn uniformly and
real catalogues are not, so 74.1% and 44.2% are upper bounds on strictness. The interval widths
are my mapping from code membership — Post lists garments per code and assigns no levels. The
value 2 itself remains uncalibrated: the run measured what it does, not whether it is right.
