## A. Spotlight effect — Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000, *JPSP* 78(2):211–222

Read in full from the published PDF. All numbers below are from the paper itself.

### A.1 What was measured

**Study 1.** 109 Cornell undergraduates: 15 targets (8 women, 7 men), 64 observers, 30 controls.
Each target was handed a T-shirt bearing a 21 × 24 cm image of Barry Manilow (pretested as
embarrassing for this population), entered a room where 2–6 observers (mode 4–5) were filling out
questionnaires, was seated, then removed after a moment. Target was asked what percentage of those
present could say who was on the shirt.

- Target estimate: **46%**. Observer accuracy: **23%**.
- Mean per-session discrepancy **23 percentage points**, 95% CI **[9, 38]**.
- Dropping the two smallest sessions (2 and 3 observers): discrepancy **27 pp**, CI **[11, 43]**.
- Targets' estimates **correlated with actual observer accuracy, r = .50, p < .05**.
- Controls who watched a staged video reenactment estimated far lower, F(2, 42) = 5.76, p < .01.
  The inflation is the felt spotlight, not a wrong general theory about observers.

**Gender, Study 1 only.** Men estimated 59%, women 35%, t(13) = 1.92, p < .10. Manilow was actually
recognised more often on a male wearer (30%) than a female one (17%). The *overestimation* did not
differ significantly: 29 pp (men) vs **18 pp (women)**, t < 1. The authors observed no gender
differences in any other study and call this possibly anomalous.

**Study 2 — the flattering shirt.** 79 participants, 15 targets. Each chose a shirt they were pleased
to wear: Bob Marley, Jerry Seinfeld, or Martin Luther King Jr. Mean self-rating of the chosen shirt
6.4 on a 9-point pride/happiness/comfort composite.

- Target estimate: **48%**. Observer accuracy: **8%**.
- Mean discrepancy **40 pp**, 95% CI **[21, 59]** — *larger* than for the embarrassing shirt.
- Estimates were **uncorrelated** with actual accuracy here (range restriction; almost nobody noticed).

**Study 3 — behaviour, not clothing.** 193 participants, 42 groups of 3–7, 20 min discussion on
inner-city policy plus 10 min drafting a signed statement. Participants overestimated their own
prominence on all six dimensions, positive and negative: speech errors t(41) = 6.84, p < .00001;
potentially offensive comments t(41) = 7.07, p < .00001; advancing the discussion t = 2.27, p < .05.
Predicted share of talking time 23.05% vs actual 20.96%. Within-group correlations between predicted
and actual standing were high (r = .34–.79, all p < .001).

**Study 4 — the mechanism.** 44 Northwestern undergraduates, Vanilla Ice shirt, confederates as
observers. Mean estimate 48%. Asked whether they had considered any other number first: 32 of 44
(73%) said yes; of the 30 who named a one-sided alternative, **23 (77%) had first considered a
*higher* number**, binomial z = 2.74, p < .01. People anchor high on their own experience and adjust
downward, insufficiently.

**Study 5 — the manipulation that matters.** 30 Northwestern undergraduates, same Manilow shirt, six
confederates. Half entered the room immediately after putting the shirt on; half after a **15-minute
delay** spent alone in a computer lab that felt public (audible staged hallway conversations, but no
actual encounters).

- Immediate condition estimate: **51%**. Delayed condition: **37%**. t(28) = 2.26, p < .05.
- Same shirt, same observers, same general beliefs about attentiveness. Only the anchor changed.

**Reverse spotlight — stated explicitly by the authors.** When behaviour is routinised and automatic,
the person is no longer focused on it, the anchor is low, and people **underestimate** how noticeable
they are. The authors' own examples: smokers underestimating how invasive the habit is to others;
heavy cologne users underestimating how readily it is detected. "Repeated exposure and habituation
can dampen the spotlight effect, and perhaps sometimes even reverse it."

**Related, same lab (Gilovich, Kruger, Medvec & Savitsky 1999, cited in the paper).** Students in a
seminar were approached on five unannounced occasions and asked how they appeared *that day* relative
to their usual appearance; classmates rated them on the same scale. Participants predicted **24% more
variability** in others' ratings than actually occurred. Day-to-day fluctuation in one's own
appearance is largely invisible to others.

### A.2 Conditions of application

**When it applies.** The person is currently focused on a *specific salient element* of their
appearance — a new item, an unusual colour, a garment outside their habitual register. Peak at the
moment of dressing and first exposure.

**How strongly.** Direction is robust: five studies, two paradigms, embarrassing and flattering
stimuli, appearance and behaviour. Magnitude is **not** a fixed multiplier. Study 1 gave 2×, Study 2
gave 6×, Study 3 was rank-based. Quote the direction, never a ratio. Absolute magnitude for
Ukrainian women is unmeasured; the closest sub-sample (women in Study 1) showed a **smaller** gap
(18 pp) and a lower own estimate (35%) than the headline figure.

**Where it breaks.**
1. **It reverses for habituated clothing.** The everyday uniform is the case where the person
   *under*estimates what others register. Reassurance is the wrong response there.
2. **It does not mean impressions are unformed.** Studies 1 and 3 both found genuine accuracy in the
   *ordering* (r = .50; within-group r = .34–.79) alongside the error in *level*. What a person
   reports about "how noticeable is this" is usable as a relative signal and unusable as an absolute
   one. This is the same shape as the project's existing separation of `сила` (self-relative, always
   present) from `надійність_виміру` — the spotlight literature independently produces that split.
3. **WEIRD sample.** Cornell and Northwestern undergraduates throughout.

### A.3 Before / after (гейт 3)

| | Without the rule | With the rule |
|---|---|---|
| New salient item, user anxious | "цей образ читається зібрано" — a platitude; user still leaves it in the wardrobe | "надягни вдома на 15 хвилин перед виходом" — the tested intervention from Study 5; it lowers the anchor, and the effect size is known (51% → 37%) |
| Habitual daily outfit | same reassurance, applied uniformly | **no reassurance**; this is the underestimation regime — the place where the system's read is worth more than the user's |
| User asks "чи це занадто помітно?" | system defers to the user's judgement | system takes the *ranking* as valid and the *level* as inflated; answers on the ranking, corrects the level |

### A.4 Маршрутизація в код

- Мовний шаблон — не одна фраза, а **дві гілки за ознакою «нова річ / звична річ»**. Ознаку профіль
  уже може дати (річ у гардеробі vs кандидат із фіду).
- Рівень сили: **`soft`** для напрямку і 15-хвилинної дії (пряме вимірювання, але WEIRD і не на
  нашій популяції). **`hint`** для будь-якої величини. **Ніколи не `default`** для множника «удвічі» —
  це артефакт одного дослідження з трьох.

## B. Скільки опцій показувати — Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman 2015, *JCP* 25(2):333–358

Read in full from the author's posted PDF, including Table 1 (all 99 observations) and Table 2.

### B.1 The finding that changes our design

**Assortment size is not the operative variable.** Across studies, with size entered as a continuous
predictor:

- linear term **b = −.005, t(37) = −1.5, p = .13**
- quadratic term **b = .002, t(36) = 1.4, p = .17**

No monotonic and no curvilinear relationship between the number of options and choice overload.
Median "small" set = 6, median "large" = 24 (M_small = 5.4, M_large = 27.8), and **the two
distributions overlap** — the same counts appear as the small set in one experiment and the large set
in another. There is no number to calibrate.

What does predict overload is four moderators, all significant at p < .001 (99 observations,
53 studies, 21 articles, N = 7202; three-level meta-regression; 68% of residual variance explained;
χ²(10) = 118.1, p < .001):

| Moderator | β | SE | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decision goal (effort-minimising) | .56 | .06 | 8.8 |
| Choice set complexity | .55 | .07 | 7.9 |
| Decision task difficulty | .37 | .08 | 4.7 |
| Preference uncertainty | .32 | .07 | 4.5 |
| Intercept (studies without moderators) | .41 | .14 | 3.0 (p = .01) |

### B.2 The sign flips — where the design leverage is

These are individual observations from Table 1. In each pair the **assortment size is identical**;
only the moderator changed, and the effect changes sign. Positive d = overload; negative d = the
larger set was *better*.

| Manipulation | Set sizes | d when present | d when absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant / easily justifiable option (Sela et al. 2009, st. 4–5) | 4 vs 12 | **−0.47, −0.46** | **+0.45, +0.48** |
| Alignable attributes (Gourville & Soman 2005, st. 1) | 2–3 vs 5–6 | **−0.43** | **+0.37** |
| Articulated ideal point (Chernev 2003a/b) | 4–8 vs 16–24 | −1.34, −0.37, −0.36, −0.35, −0.29, −0.30 | +0.91, +0.72, +0.57, +0.47, +0.16, +0.17 |
| High expertise (Morrin et al. 2012) | 9 vs 21 | **−0.56** | **+0.33** |
| Verbal presentation (Townsend & Kahn 2014) | 8 vs 27 | **−0.32** (verbal) | **+0.37** (visual) |
| Few attributes (Greifeneder et al. 2010) | 6 vs 30 | −0.12, −0.28 | +0.81, +0.54 |
| Browsing rather than buying (Oppewal & Koelemeijer 2005) | 5–6 vs 11–12 | **−0.82** | — |

Operational reading for a garment recommender:

1. **Mark one option as the justified pick.** The single largest documented sign flip at constant set
   size. Do not shorten the list to achieve this; mark within it.
2. **Keep candidates alignable.** Options that differ in the *level* of the same attribute (three
   midi skirts differing in lightness) are alignable; options that differ by presence/absence of a
   feature (skirt vs trousers vs dress) are not. Within one colour window our candidates are already
   alignable by construction — this is a condition we satisfy, not a task.
3. **Reduce the number of attributes shown per option**, not the number of options.
4. **Visual presentation is the overload-producing format.** Townsend & Kahn found a photo grid
   produced overload where the same set described verbally did not. This is uncomfortable for a
   fashion product and is exactly the kind of claim the стенд can test.
5. **Preference articulation flips the sign**, and this is the moderator that maps onto our profile —
   but the mapping is our conjecture, not something Chernev measured.

### B.3 The dispute the corpus flattened

Тема-8 currently states the main effect as settled at ≈ 0, citing Scheibehenne, Greifeneder & Todd
2010. That is one side of a live disagreement:

- Chernev **replicates** the null when moderators are excluded: t(20) = −.10, p = .48.
- With moderators in the model, the intercept is **significant**: .41, t = 3.0, p = .01.
- Restricted to studies where the task was choosing an option *from* a given assortment (excluding
  choice *among* assortments): **b = .17, t(39) = 4.5, p < .001**.
- Split by design: studies without moderators **b = .41, t(39) = 5.3, p < .001**; studies with
  moderators **b = .04, t(39) = .9, p = .39**.
- Chernev's reading: aggregating across moderator levels cancels the effect. He states plainly that
  the significant main effect is "counter to the data reported by prior meta-analytic research."
- Data differ too: Scheibehenne used 63 observations / 50 experiments / N = 5036 including
  unpublished work; Chernev used 99 / 53 / 7202, published only, and covers 78% of Scheibehenne's
  published observations. By Chernev's recalculation Scheibehenne's model explains 36% of variance
  against his 68%. No publication bias detected in Chernev's set (funnel asymmetry z = .34, p = .74).

**Consequence for force level.** Any claim about "choice overload" as a *main effect* — in either
direction — cannot exceed **`hint`**. The corpus currently carries the null side at `soft` with a
one-sided citation. That is exactly the failure the project's own invariant forbids: force above
verified scope.

### B.4 Where it breaks

Every study in the meta-analysis is a Western consumer-goods lab experiment: jam, chocolate, pens,
essays, MP3 players, cameras, golf balls, crackers, mutual funds, flowers, hotel resorts. **None on
apparel. None with a person profile in the loop.** Chernev's own stated limits: the four moderators
were tested in isolation, never in interaction; three-factor designs are explicitly named as future
work; the decision-goal moderator rests on the fewest studies of the four.

### B.5 R-PSY-03 переписати

Чинне формулювання: «**обсяг курації ∝ ясності вподобань користувача**» — побудоване навколо
кількості опцій. Первинник каже, що кількість не є важелем (b = −.005, p = .13). Правило написане
навколо не тієї змінної.

Заміна:

> **R-PSY-03 [soft · presentation, not filtering].** Overload is driven by set complexity, task
> difficulty, preference uncertainty and decision goal — not by option count (Chernev et al. 2015:
> no linear or quadratic effect of size). Therefore: never truncate the candidate list to a target
> number. Instead (a) mark exactly one candidate as the justified pick, (b) keep candidates alignable
> within a slot, (c) show fewer attributes per candidate, (d) prefer verbal framing when the set is
> large. Count may expand freely once the profile carries an articulated ideal point.
> *Breaks:* no apparel study exists; the profile→ideal-point mapping is our conjecture; the
> visual/verbal flip rests on a single study (n = 129 / 107).

## C. Кодові дельти

**1. `feed.покриття` — домінантна опція рахується не тоді, коли треба.**

Зараз `найкращий = min(у_слоті, key=відстань_до_вікна)` обчислюється **лише коли вікно порожнє**.
Це навпаки: позначка потрібна саме тоді, коли кандидатів багато.

```python
# зараз
рядок = dict(..., приклади=[c["назва"][:46] for c in придатні[:3]], порожньо=len(придатні)==0)
if not придатні:
    найкращий = min(у_слоті, key=...)   # ← лише в порожній гілці
```

Дельта: обчислювати ранг завжди, віддавати позначену опцію, `приклади` не різати довільним `[:3]`,
а віддавати впорядковано з явним `рекомендована`. Різати список — не наша операція; позначати — наша.

**2. `конвеєр.г["кандидати"]` — додати поле `рекомендована` на кожне вікно** і не додавати жодного
ліміту кількості. Планована фіча «скільки опцій показати за артикульованістю» — **знімається**:
первинник каже, що це не та змінна.

**3. Гардрейл R-PSY-02 (жодної універсальної семантики кольору) — перевірено, порушень нема.**
Грепнув `palette.py`, `colorspace.py`, `outfit.py`, `статус.py` на «довіра / авторитет / пристрасть /
спокій / романтичний / владний» — жодного входження в поясненнях. Правило вже дотримане кодом;
це покриття, а не робота, і я його таким і подаю.

## D. Один фальсифікований наступний крок

Прогнати на наявному стенді маніпуляцію домінантної опції як окрему вісь, на тому самому наборі
кандидатів і тій самій людині:

- арм **D+**: набір із рівно однією позначеною опцією («рекомендовано, бо …»)
- арм **D−**: той самий набір, без позначки

Передбачення з Sela et al. 2009: різниця близько **d ≈ 0.9** (−0.47 проти +0.45) на частці вибору
й відкладенні рішення. **Якщо на n, достатньому для виявлення d = 0.5, різниці нема — модератор не
переноситься на одяг, і R-PSY-03 помирає повністю**, а не переписується. Це єдине твердження в
цьому файлі, яке ми можемо перевірити самі, не купуючи нових джерел.
