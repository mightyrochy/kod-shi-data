# Theme 2 — Colour. Unified knowledge.

Supersedes the previous `тема-2_колір.md` and `colour-primary-sources-EN.md`. Single file,
no supplements. 42 rules: 37 carried over, 5 added from the primary-source pass.

**Language.** English, because all sources are English-language — Chevreul in Martel's
translation, CIE, Munsell/OSA, Pointer, Ratnasingam & Anderson, Radeloff, Stacchio.
Ukrainian is kept for code identifiers (`палітра`, `вікно_нейтралі_за_температурою`) and
for garment names, which are catalogue terms.

## How to read a rule

Every rule carries: **claim / when it holds / how strongly / where it breaks / tier /
force / code site**. A rule with no code site is vocabulary, not an executable rule, and
is marked so. Tiers T1–T4 by source quality. Force: hard / strong / default / soft / hint.
**Strength invariant: force may never exceed tier.** A conclusion without conditions is a
truism; in styling all the value is in the conditions.

## Status audit — read this before trusting any rule below

Each rule was checked mechanically for three things: does it carry a **magnitude**, a
**failure condition**, and a **code site**. Two of three = working knowledge. One or zero =
a note about knowledge, which fails the reread test — you would have to go back to the
source to apply it.

## Chevreul audit — all 11 rules run against the original text

Source used: Martel translation, 3rd edition, George Bell & Sons, 1890 (Getty copy,
archive.org `principlesofharm00chev`), full text. Chevreul's own numbered paragraphs.

| rule | verdict against original |
|---|---|
| R-CHEV-01 | ✅ clean — §8, §16 as cited |
| R-CHEV-02 | ✅ clean — complementary pairs transcribed exactly, §6 |
| R-CHEV-03 | ⚠️ **incomplete** — lost the spatial decay law → R-CHEV-15 |
| R-CHEV-04 | ✅ clean — and the source **confirms** this pass's correction |
| R-CHEV-05 | ⚠️ **incomplete** — lost mixed contrast entirely → R-CHEV-17 |
| R-CHEV-06 | ⚠️ page citation unverifiable + lost a whole mechanism → R-CHEV-16 |
| R-CHEV-07 | ⚠️ page citation unverifiable |
| R-CHEV-08 | ❌ **context loss** — see below |
| R-CHEV-09 | ✅ clean — six harmonies in two groups, exactly as recorded |
| R-CHEV-10 | ⚠️ **incomplete** — lost the gating condition |
| R-CHEV-11 | ✅ clean — Part II §III ch.I §1 and §3, pp.266 / 273 |

**R-CHEV-04 confirmed by the original.** Chevreul states that grey bodies contiguous to
coloured bodies exhibit contrast of colour more strikingly than black or white do — grey as
the body **receiving** the effect. This pass's correction (grey is the target, not the
surround) was made on inference from the induction literature; the original text confirms it
independently.

**R-CHEV-10 lost its gating condition.** The original states that the complementary
association is the only one in which colours mutually improve, strengthen and purify each
other without leaving their scales — **and that the tones must be nearly of equal
intensity.** The corpus recorded the benefit and dropped the condition. It also dropped two
extensions: the association still holds when the colours are not exactly complementary, and
when both are tarnished with grey. Condition added; force unchanged.

**R-CHEV-08 — the largest context loss in the theme.** The rule is the two-goal frame
(heighten vs dissimulate) and it is the most load-bearing idea in the layer: the reason the
system carries a goal instead of a verdict. Against the original:

- The corpus cites pp.257–258. In this edition the passage is at **pp.286–287** (Part II,
  §III, ch.II, §3, "1st Supposition — the painter wishes to heighten the tint of a
  complexion"; "2nd Supposition — the painter wishes to dissimulate a tint of the
  complexion"). Editions repaginate, so this alone is not proof of error — but the corpus
  page numbers cannot be confirmed against any edition I can reach, and should be recorded
  as unverified rather than as citations.
- **The subject of both propositions is the painter, not the dresser.** Chevreul is telling
  a portraitist how to represent a complexion, not telling a woman what to wear.
- Both propositions sit inside §3, **"the dress of women with black or olive skins."** The
  frame the corpus generalized to everyone is stated in the source for one skin group.

The two-goal frame is still the right architecture — but its warrant is not "Chevreul said
so about dressing." It is a painter's technique, generalized by us. **Tier T2/T3 → T3;
force stays `default` because the architecture earns its place from decisions, not from the
source.** Recorded honestly rather than dropped.

### R-CHEV-15 — [new] Induction is edge-localized and decays with distance from contact.

**Source.** Chevreul §10 and §11, with his own experimental protocol. §10: the modification
is not equally intense across the whole surface — it is strongest at the line of contact and
weakens progressively away from it; he demonstrates this by masking, showing stripe pairs at
increasing distance are progressively less modified. He also shows **contiguity is not
required**: masking the touching stripes still leaves the next ones modified. §11: ten flat
grey stripes of increasing depth, viewed together, stop looking flat — each appears shaded
from edge to edge, so the set reads as channeled grooves rather than plane surfaces.

**What the corpus lost.** R-CHEV-03 was recorded as a binary claim — lighter looks lighter.
The original claim is a **gradient**: maximum at the seam, decaying outward, and still
present at a gap. That is the entire spatial structure of the effect, and it was dropped.

**Decision it changes.** `palette.py` weights induction by `near_вага`, proximity of the
**slot** to the face — a per-garment constant. The source says the governing variable is
**distance from the contact line**, which is a different quantity: a collar and an open
neckline in the same slot have very different contact geometry. A scarf knotted at the
throat and a jacket lapel are both "near," and the scarf's contact line is at the jaw while
the lapel's is at the collarbone.

**Where it breaks.** Chevreul gives no distance units — his decay is qualitative, shown by
masking at roughly 12-inch separations on 20-inch sheets, at unspecified viewing distance.
So the **shape** (monotone decay, non-zero at a gap) is T1; **any numeric falloff constant
is folkloric** until measured. Do not encode a radius.
· T1 for the shape, T4 for any constant · default · `palette.near_вага`

### R-CHEV-16 — [new] Inter-reflection: the garment throws its own light on the skin.

**This mechanism was absent from the corpus entirely.** Chevreul treats it separately from
induction, in Part II §III ch.II §1 Article 4: the head-dress considered "in relation to the
coloured rays which it may reflect upon the skin" (p.279). It is a distinct article from
Article 3, which covers complexion and contiguous drapery under simultaneous contrast.
**Chevreul separates the two mechanisms; the corpus collapsed them into one.**

**Why this matters more than any other line in this file.** There are now three competing
predictions for what a garment does to adjacent skin:

| mechanism | direction of skin shift | status |
|---|---|---|
| complementary law (old corpus) | toward the garment's **complement** | superseded — R-CHEV-02 |
| direction law (modern) | **away from** the garment, along the line | best perceptual model |
| **inter-reflection** (Chevreul, recovered) | **toward the garment's own hue** | **physical, not perceptual** |

Inter-reflection is light transport. It is not a perceptual illusion, so **nothing in the
Ratnasingam & Anderson critique touches it.** It is computable from garment reflectance,
geometry and illumination, and it is the only one of the three that a camera would record —
the other two exist only in the observer.

**It resolves the orange anomaly.** This file's R-CHEV-07 audit found that orange produces
essentially zero *hue induction* on skin (strength 0.03) because it sits on the skin's own
hue line — while every practitioner reports orange draining a complexion. Inter-reflection
predicts orange bounces orange onto skin, raising skin b\* and chroma without moving hue.
The observed phenomenon is real; it was filed under the wrong mechanism for the whole
history of this corpus.

**Where it breaks.** Magnitude depends on garment reflectance, the solid angle it subtends
at the skin, surface finish (a matte wool bounces diffusely; a satin bounces a specular
highlight), and ambient illumination. Under strong frontal light the ambient dominates and
inter-reflection is negligible; under soft or side light it is not. **No magnitude is given
by Chevreul and none has been measured here.** The mechanism is T1 (it is physics); every
number about it is currently T4.

**Decision it changes.** Two garments identical in Lab but different in finish — matte crepe
vs satin — currently score identically everywhere in the pipeline. Under this mechanism they
do not, and the difference lands on the face. This is also the first place the colour layer
and the materials layer (тема-3) touch.
· T1 mechanism, T4 magnitude · **hint** until measured · no code site yet — **this is the
gap, not a fix**

### R-CHEV-17 — [new] Mixed contrast — the corpus records two of Chevreul's three.

Chevreul distinguishes **three**: simultaneous (two colours side by side), successive (the
eye calls up the complement after fixation), and **mixed** — the complement called up by the
eye being added to the next colour actually looked at. The corpus has R-CHEV-01 and
R-CHEV-05 and no third.

**When it applies here.** Mixed contrast is the mechanism of **sequential viewing**: scrolling
a catalogue, flipping between try-on renders, looking from one outfit to the next. Every
judgement a user makes in the app is made under mixed contrast, not simultaneous contrast —
they are looking at outfit N having just looked at outfit N−1.

**Where it breaks.** Chevreul gives no decay time. Modern afterimage work puts the useful
window at seconds, so it matters for rapid A/B comparison and not for considered viewing.
This is a **testing-stand** concern before it is a styling concern: the blind A/B/C stand
shows arms in sequence, and arm order may therefore bias verdicts.
· T1 mechanism, T4 magnitude · hint · **affects `pair_test.py` ordering, not the composer**

## Munsell audit — all 7 rules run against the renotation data

Source: Newhall, S. M., Nickerson, D. & Judd, D. B. (1943), Final Report of the O.S.A.
Subcommittee on the Spacing of the Munsell Colors, *JOSA* 33(7):385–418. Audited against the
machine-readable renotation — **2734 real chips**, converted to CIELAB under Illuminant C /
2° as the renotation defines them. This audit is numeric, not textual.

| rule | verdict |
|---|---|
| R-MUN-01 | ⚠️ **half true** — ordering independent, ranges hard-coupled |
| R-MUN-02 | ✅ confirmed, with a systematic bias worth knowing |
| R-MUN-03 | ⚠️ same defect as R-MUN-01 |
| R-MUN-04 | ✅ **confirmed numerically** — and understated |
| R-MUN-05 | ⚠️ inherits R-MUN-01's defect |
| R-MUN-06 | ✅ **confirmed, and now quantified** — the strongest rule in the family |
| R-MUN-07 | ✅ confirmed — renotation is Illuminant C, not D65 |

**R-MUN-02 — Value ≈ L\*/10. Confirmed: r = 0.9999.** But not identical: L\*/10 runs
systematically **high by +0.12 Value units on average, peaking at +0.17 at V = 5–6** — that
is, 1.7 L\* units, and the peak sits at mid-lightness, exactly where faces (L\*≈66 → V≈6.4)
and mid-tone garments live. The bias is systematic, not noise (SD 0.041). It is small enough
to ignore for ordering and too large to ignore if L\*/10 is ever printed to a user as a
Munsell value. Condition added; force unchanged.

**R-MUN-04 — the asymmetric solid. Confirmed, and the corpus understated it.** Peak chroma
by hue family, from the data:

| family | peaks at V | max C | | family | peaks at V | max C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y (yellow) | **8** | 20 | | PB (violet-blue) | **1** | **38** |
| GY | 8 | 24 | | P | 3 | 34 |
| YR | 7 | 20 | | RP | 4 | 26 |
| G | 5 | 28 | | R (red) | **4** | 20 |
| B (blue) | 5 | **18** | | BG | 4 | 24 |

The corpus claim — yellows peak high, blues and reds peak lower — is **confirmed**:
Y at V=8, PB at V=1, R at V=4. What the corpus missed is the size of the hue-to-hue
variation: **max chroma ranges 18 (B) to 38 (PB), a 2.1× spread, between adjacent hue
families.** Blue and violet-blue sit next to each other on the wheel and differ by 2× in
reachable chroma. "Equally saturated across hues" is not merely approximate — it is
unachievable, and the error is largest exactly where the corpus assumed a smooth wheel.

**R-MUN-06 — the axes are not commensurable. Now with a number.** Cost of one step on each
axis, in ΔE00, measured over every adjacent chip pair in the renotation:

| axis | median ΔE00 | mean | SD | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value, ΔV = 1 | **8.02** | 8.32 | 1.20 | 6.33 | 12.1 |
| Chroma, ΔC = 2 | 3.27 | 3.68 | 1.73 | 1.06 | 9.5 |
| Hue, Δ = one 2.5-step | 3.55 | 3.82 | 1.93 | 0.66 | **16.7** |

**One Value step costs 2.5× a Chroma step and 2.3× a Hue step.** Anything that treats "a
step of value" as comparable to "a step of chroma" is wrong by a factor of two and a half.

And the corpus's own hedge — *steps are roughly even within an axis* — is **false for hue**:
hue steps range from 0.66 to 16.7 ΔE00, a **25× spread within a single axis**. Value is the
only well-behaved axis (6.33–12.1, under 2×).

**R-MUN-01 / R-MUN-03 — "three independent axes" is half true.** The axes are independently
*ordered*, which is what Munsell claimed. Their *ranges* are hard-coupled: the chroma ceiling
falls monotonically with value across the whole solid —

```
V=1 C*max 38 │ V=4  32 │ V=7  26
V=2       38 │ V=5  30 │ V=8  24
V=3       34 │ V=6  28 │ V=9  20
```

— a 1.9× change in available chroma driven entirely by value. So "chroma is independent of
value" holds as a statement about perceptual ordering and fails as a statement about range.
The corpus asserts the first and the code uses it as if it were the second.

### The unit trap, demonstrated by falling into it

R-MUN-06 exists to prevent one specific error. While running this audit I made it: I compared
Munsell chroma against Pointer's CIELAB C\* ceiling and read a 38-vs-115 gap as a
contradiction between sources. It is not a contradiction — **Munsell Chroma and CIELAB C\*
are different units.** Measured across all 2734 chips:

| | C\* per unit of Munsell Chroma |
|---|---|
| median | **4.86** |
| range | 2.55 – 9.07 — a **3.6× spread** |
| by hue family | B 4.32 … Y **7.28** (yellow needs 1.7× more C\* per Munsell step than blue) |
| by value | V=1 4.79 … V=9 **6.37** |

**There is no single conversion factor.** Anyone who multiplies Munsell chroma by a constant
to reach C\* is wrong by up to 3.6×, and worst on yellows — the family most common in warm
autumn retail. R-MUN-06 goes from `default` to **`hard`**: this is not a caution, it is a
trap with a measured depth, and I walked into it inside this audit.

**R-MUN-07 — confirmed and sharper than recorded.** The renotation is defined under
**Illuminant C**, not D65. This is the same illuminant mismatch already flagged for Pointer
in R-COL-12. Two of the theme's thirteen sources — the two that supply every chroma ceiling
in the file — are both Illuminant C, and the pipeline is D65. **The mismatch is not
per-source; it is systematic across the whole gamut layer**, and it has not been corrected
anywhere. This is now the single most-repeated unquantified error in the colour layer.

## CIE / Sharma audit — running code tested, not documents

**R-COL-02 (formula) — `colorspace.de00` run against Sharma, Wu & Dalal (2005) test data.**
34 pairs, published specifically to catch implementation errors. **34/34 pass, max deviation
4.95 × 10⁻⁵.** Symmetry `de00(a,b) == de00(b,a)` holds exactly. Pairs 9–16 — the hue-angle
discontinuity at a\*≈0, where a silently wrong implementation still looks plausible — all
pass. This is the only rule in the theme verified against running code rather than prose.
· ✅ **T1 confirmed**

**R-COL-01 — perceptual space over sRGB. Confirmed, and it earns its `hard`.** Over 190 pairs
of the base wardrobe, ΔE00 vs Euclidean sRGB distance correlates r = 0.853 — high enough to
look safe, and it is not: **rank disagreement reaches 103 positions out of 190.** Worst
offenders are exactly the styling-relevant pairs:

| pair | ΔE00 rank | sRGB rank | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| бордо + хвойний | 123 | 20 | sRGB calls them close; perceptually they are far |
| гірчиця + фуксія | 164 | 80 | sRGB halves the real distance |
| гірчиця + молочний | 61 | 141 | sRGB calls them far; perceptually they are close |

A pipeline sorting garment similarity in sRGB would put бордо+хвойний in the top 10% most
similar when it sits mid-pack. · ✅ **T1 confirmed, `hard` earned**

**R-COL-07 — ΔE00 is non-metric and out of range. Confirmed, and worse than recorded.**

*Non-metric:* over 3420 ordered triples of the base wardrobe, **102 violate the triangle
inequality (3.0%)**. Worst case is the most-used chain in the whole system:
білий → сірий → чорний measures 66.62, while білий → чорний measured directly is **85.13** —
an excess of 18.50 ΔE00. The neutral chain the tone-on-tone gate reasons over is precisely
where the metric breaks.

*Out of range:* the corpus records that the formula is best below ΔE00 ≈ 5. Measured:
**100% of the 190 wardrobe pairs exceed 5**, median **35.3** — seven times the validity
ceiling. Every outfit-level ΔE00 call in the pipeline is outside the range the formula was
fitted for. It remains valid where differences are small — catalogue ingest, same-colour
matching — and that is the only place it should carry weight.

**This resolves the R-COL-02 / R-COL-07 conflict properly.** The formula is T1 and verified;
the thresholds are folkloric (R-COL-02); and the whole apparatus is out of range for
outfit-scale work (R-COL-07). All three are true simultaneously, and the pipeline currently
acts as if only the first were. · ✅ **T1 confirmed, quantified**

## Radeloff audit — the corpus cites this source in the wrong direction

This is the most serious error found in the whole audit, and it sits under the theme's most
heavily enforced position.

**What the corpus claims.** R-PC-01 (force **hard**), R-PC-05 and R-PC-06 cite Radeloff
1990/1991 as the evidence that seasonal prescriptions have weak, inconsistent support — the
foundation of the anti-seasonal stance that R-PC-04 enforces 15 times in code.

**What Radeloff 1990 actually concluded** (*Role of Color in Perception of Attractiveness*,
*Perceptual and Motor Skills* 71:151–160, 276 subjects): <cite index="13-1">subjects significantly concurred with expert choices of recommended and non-recommended colours in five of the six photograph sets, and the study is presented as lending credence that wearing recommended colours makes a difference to judgements of what looks best among subjects over the age of 12.</cite>

That is a **positive** result for personal colour recommendation. The corpus cites it as
negative evidence.

**What Radeloff 1991 actually concluded** (*Psychological Types, Color Attributes, and Color
Preferences*, 111 students): <cite index="14-1">summer and winter colours were significantly preferred over autumn colours, spring over autumn; and the study is presented as supporting the theory that hue seasonal categories, value and chroma can be used to elicit variations in human response to colour.</cite> Also framed as supportive.

**What this does and does not overturn.** Being fair to both sides:

- Radeloff 1990 tested whether observers agree that expert-recommended colours look better.
  It did **not** test inter-analyst reliability — whether two analysts assign you the same
  season. R-PC-01's reliability claim is therefore **unsourced**, not contradicted.
- The 1990 design carries a confound the corpus should have caught in either direction: if
  experts pick colours that are better on general grounds — contrast level, saturation,
  value match — then observer agreement validates *the experts*, not *the four-season
  system*. Agreement with a practitioner is not validation of the theory the practitioner
  cites. This is the cleanest available example of the corpus's own rule 5 (practitioner
  decisions outrank cited theory) — the data support the decisions and say nothing about
  the theory.
- Radeloff 1991 measures **preference**, not what flatters. Design students preferring
  summer palettes over autumn is a taste finding, confounded with fashion cycle and cohort.
  It cannot support or undermine a flattery claim in either direction.
- The anti-seasonal position **survives on other evidence**: the ~55% ML ceiling with
  inconsistent expert labels (R-PC-07, Stacchio 2024) is independent and stands.

**Verdict.** R-PC-01's conclusion may well be right; its citation is wrong in sign.
**Force `hard` → `default`** until the reliability claim is sourced to a study that actually
measured reliability. Per the strength invariant, a rule cannot hold `hard` on a citation
that points the other way. R-PC-05 and R-PC-06 lose Radeloff as support and rest on
R-PC-07 alone.

**This is also a genealogy failure.** Three rules cited one author twice and counted it as an
evidence base. Two papers by one researcher are one voice, and in this case the voice was
saying something else.

## The five rules with no source at all

R-CHEV-12, R-COL-04, R-COL-05, R-COL-06 and R-PC-02 are derived — compositions of other
rules, or judgements the corpus made. There is nothing to run them against, so the audit
question is different: **do they follow from their inputs?**

| rule | follows? | note |
|---|---|---|
| R-COL-04 | ✅ yes | composition of R-COL-02 + R-CHEV-02; correctly updated this pass |
| R-COL-05 | ✅ yes | follows from R-CHEV-09; correctly held at `hint` |
| R-PC-02 | ✅ yes | follows from R-PC-01/03/05 — but inherits the Radeloff defect above |
| R-CHEV-12 | ⚠️ **partly** | see below |
| R-COL-06 | ❌ **over-forced** | carries `hard` with no source and no magnitude |

**R-COL-06 is the clearest strength-invariant violation in the file.** It says image colour
is unreliable, carries force `hard`, cites nothing, and gives no error size — while the
number exists and is sitting in the codebase (smartphone vs spectrophotometer ΔE00 ≈ 7.6,
Niakan 2025, already in `colorspace.py`). A `hard` rule with no source is authority without
evidence, which is the exact failure mode the tier system exists to prevent. **Move the
Niakan number into the rule and re-tier to T2** — the force is defensible once the
evidence is attached; it is not defensible while the rule is empty.
**Done, run 3 (2026-09-06), with one correction to this very recommendation:** T2 does not
license `hard`. The code ceiling `outfit.СТЕЛЯ_ЗА_ТІРОМ` maps T2 → `strong`, and the rule was
set to `strong`. Recommending "T2 · `hard`" was itself a small instance of force exceeding
verification.

**R-CHEV-12 is partly circular.** It states that seasonal taxonomy is convention rather than
mechanism, and carries `hard`. Its stated support is R-PC-06, whose support was Radeloff —
now withdrawn. What remains is R-PC-07 (the 55% ML ceiling), which is real and independent.
So R-CHEV-12 survives, but on one leg instead of two, and the corpus recorded it as though
the legs were independent. Force held at `hard` because the guardrail is a **prohibition** —
it prevents the system asserting a season, and the cost of being wrong in that direction is
asymmetric — but the redundancy the corpus claimed is not there.

## Stacchio / Su audit — the number is right, the reading is not

**Confirmed as recorded.** Stacchio, Paolanti, Spigarelli & Frontoni (2024), *Deep Armocromia*,
ECCV 2024 Workshops, Springer LNCS 15623, pp.352–367. <cite index="19-1">FaRL64 fine-tuned establishes a baseline of 0.554 accuracy on four-season classification</cite>, hierarchical
design predicting season first and then twelve sub-types. The corpus transcribed the figure
correctly. ✅

**Three things the corpus never recorded, all of which change the argument.**

**1. The error is not uniform across seasons — and this rescues R-PC-03.** From the Lumina
benchmark on the same dataset: <cite index="19-1">of 203 Spring samples only 79 are correctly classified, with 59 predicted as Summer, 43 as Autumn and 22 as Winter, while of 264 Winter samples 180 are correct with errors spread thinly.</cite>

| class | correct | rate | vs 25% chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring | 79 / 203 | **38.9%** | 1.56× |
| Winter | 180 / 264 | **68.2%** | **2.7×** |

The single "~55%" figure hides a **1.75× spread**. Winter — the deep, high-contrast class —
is substantially recoverable; Spring is near noise. **What the model can actually see is
depth and contrast, not season.** That is R-PC-03's claim, arriving from an independent
direction, and the corpus had the data to notice and did not.

**2. Top-2 accuracy is 0.843.** <cite index="19-1">The top-2 accuracy of 0.843 suggests the system remains practically useful</cite> even where the top-1 verdict fails. The corpus
recorded only the top-1 number, which supports "do not ship a verdict" — correct — but
misses the constructive alternative the same data offers: **narrowing to two candidates and
letting the person choose is well supported.** That is a product decision the corpus could
have made two audits ago and did not, because it recorded only the number that argued its
case.

**3. The diagnosis in the corpus is not the diagnosis in the source.** R-PC-07 reads 0.554
as evidence that expert labels are inconsistent — that the target itself is noisy, therefore
seasons are weakly real. The source names a different leading cause: <cite index="19-1">lighting normalization is the most pressing unresolved issue, because the Deep Armocromia dataset aggregates images under uncontrolled illumination.</cite>

These are different claims with opposite implications. Inconsistent labels would mean the
category is not real. Uncontrolled lighting means **the measurement is bad and says nothing
about whether the category is real.** The corpus picked the reading that supported its
position.

**This is the second citation-direction problem in the R-PC family**, after Radeloff. Both
run the same way: a source is enrolled as evidence for the anti-seasonal stance while
actually saying something adjacent or contrary. The stance may still be right — the honest
position now is that **it is substantially less well-evidenced than the corpus claimed**, and
that R-PC-07 does not carry it alone as I stated one pass ago. It carries a weaker version:
*automatic season assignment from uncontrolled photographs is unreliable* — which is a claim
about our input pipeline, not about the taxonomy.

**R-PC-07 re-tiered: T1 → T2, force `default` unchanged.** The number is T1; the inference
drawn from it is T2 at best.

**R-COL-09 and R-COL-10 confirmed, and strengthened.** The Lumina pipeline segments eleven
facial regions via Facer/FaRL before extracting LAB features — direct corroboration of
parse-then-extract (R-COL-10). And the lighting finding above is the strongest available
support for R-COL-09: illuminant normalization is not hygiene, it is **the leading named
cause of failure** in the only published benchmark on this task. ✅ both confirmed

## Ottosson audit — and a correction to my own R-MUN-06 finding

R-COL-08 claims OKLab is simpler and more uniform than CIELAB for gradients and clustering.
Ottosson 2020 is a blog post, not peer-reviewed; the corpus tiered it T2, which is generous.
Rather than audit the prose, I tested the claim against the Munsell renotation — a space
built to have perceptually equal steps, so a uniform colour space should return equal
distances for equal Munsell steps. Metric: coefficient of variation of step size, lower is
more uniform.

| axis | CIELAB CV | OKLab CV | winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| value | 0.095 | **0.031** | **OKLab, 67% more even** |
| chroma | **0.199** | 0.270 | CIELAB, 26% |
| hue | **0.737** | 0.791 | CIELAB, 7% |

**The corpus claim is half right, and the half matters.** OKLab is decisively better on the
lightness axis and worse on chroma and hue. Since the pipeline's heaviest colour work is
value reasoning — contrast level, tone-on-tone, the whole R-CHEV-03 axis — **OKLab is the
better space for exactly the part the system uses most**, and the worse space for catalogue
clustering. Held at `hint`, which was the right call, for a reason the corpus never stated.

### Correction to R-MUN-06 — the metric, not the space, unbalances the axes

Two audits ago I reported that Munsell's axes are non-commensurable: one Value step costs
2.5× a Chroma step. Measured in **ΔE00**. Measured in **raw CIELAB Euclidean distance** on
the same chips:

| space | value / chroma | value / hue |
|---|---|---|
| CIELAB, Euclidean | **1.01** | 1.45 |
| OKLab, Euclidean | 3.14 | 3.94 |
| CIELAB, ΔE00 | **2.50** | 2.30 |

**In raw CIELAB the Munsell axes are almost perfectly balanced — 1.01.** The 2.5× imbalance
is introduced by CIEDE2000's own weighting, chiefly the S_C term that discounts chroma
differences at high chroma. Munsell built the axes well; the metric unbalances them.

So R-MUN-06's "axes not commensurable" is **true in ΔE00 and false in raw CIELAB**, and the
corpus stated it without naming a metric. Corrected in the rule. My own earlier report
carried the same defect and is corrected here rather than quietly amended — the finding was
right about the number and wrong about the cause.

## Li et al. 2025 — the harmony layer measured on real garments

Li, H.-C., Wang, L.-K., Chang, Y.-K. & Huang, K.-Y. (2025). Establishing colour harmony
evaluation and recommendation model for clothing colour matching. *Fashion and Textiles*
12:27. DOI 10.1186/s40691-025-00433-y. Open access, CC BY. Full text read.

**Method.** 20 observers (10M/10F), mean age **20.0, SD 0.94**. 500 full-body two-piece
outfit images from DeepFashion-Multimodal. Binary harmony/disharmony judgement. Dark room,
20-min dark adaptation, 60 cm viewing distance, EIZO CS2731 calibrated with an i1Basic Pro 3
to D65 at 120 cd/m², neutral grey surround explicitly to suppress simultaneous contrast.
Each observer ran the full set **twice, one week apart**; inter- and intra-observer
consistency checked by Hamming distance and Jaccard coefficient before analysis.

### Finding 1 — every published harmony model is uncorrelated with modern judgement

| model | correlation with observers |
|---|---|
| Moon & Spencer aesthetic index (1944) | **0.166** |
| Huang et al. 2017, built specifically for two-piece garments | **0.11** |
| Ou et al. 2018, "universal" colour harmony | **−0.02** |

Ou's model is not merely weak — it is **uncorrelated**. The authors attribute this to it not
being designed for clothing and taking no account of area proportion. Huang's model, which
*was* built for two-piece garments, does better and still explains essentially nothing; the
authors attribute the residual gap to age, since Huang split observers at 45 and found the
groups perceive harmony differently, while this panel was all about 20.

**This is the measurement R-COL-05 never had.** The rule has stood at `hint` with the note
that hue relation is a feature and not a verdict. That was the right call, and it can now be
stated with a number instead of a hedge: **no published harmony model predicts clothing
judgements above r = 0.17.**

### Finding 2 — tone-on-tone is the rule modern observers agree with least

The authors name three rules where contemporary perception diverges most sharply: NCS
relational consistency, **tone-on-tone**, and the Moon & Spencer index. NCS *attribute*
consistency showed the highest agreement of any rule tested.

**This collides with my own proposed next step and I am not going to bury it.** R-COL-11
(kL=2) was justified by showing that three neutral pairs move from "visible mismatch" into
the tone-on-tone band — presented as a fix. Li et al. find tone-on-tone is precisely where
traditional rules and modern viewers part company. The two findings are compatible — kL=2 is
a claim about **the metric**, tone-on-tone divergence is a claim about **the verdict** — but
together they say something the earlier pass did not: **making more garment pairs land in the
tone-on-tone band may be moving the system toward the least-trusted rule in the set.** The
regression test stands; its interpretation changes. Passing it confirms the metric behaves as
modelled, not that the resulting outfits are better.

### Finding 3 — the operational definitions, which the corpus has never carried

Every one of these is executable and none is currently in the codebase:

| concept | Li et al. operational definition |
|---|---|
| **achromatic** | **C\* < 5** |
| **tone** | lightness **×** chroma (a product, not a pair) |
| tonal harmony | Δhue 10–30° **and** Δtone 5–25 |
| tone-on-tone | Δhue < 10°, tones differ |
| tone-in-tone | Δtone < 5, hues differ |
| NCS attribute consistency | Δhue ≤ 30, ΔL ≤ 7.5, ΔC ≤ 3.5 |
| NCS relational consistency | ratio difference < 0.01 |
| NCS lightness consistency | ΔL ≤ 5 |
| Munsell area rule | area ratio inversely proportional to lightness × chroma product |

**`ACHROMATIC_C = 2.0` in `colorspace.py` is confirmed wrong by an independent source.**
The published clothing implementation is C\* < 5. This was already flagged in the corpus from
a different direction; Li et al. corroborate it in a garment context with a working
pipeline behind it. Fix the constant.

**"Tone" as lightness × chroma is a definition the system does not have at all.** The
codebase reasons about L\* and C\* separately everywhere. Three of the nine harmony rules are
defined on their product, and one of them — tonal — is the only rule in the set with a
two-sided window on both axes.

### Finding 4 — harmony barely discriminates on real clothing

Rule prevalence over the 9,056 real two-piece outfits: 4,315 / 5,291 / 537 / 876 / 1,329 /
1,009 / 1,039 → **47.6%, 58.4%, 5.9%, 9.7%, 14.7%, 11.1%, 11.5%**.

Two rules fire on roughly half the catalogue. By our own standard those are not filters.

Worse for the whole layer: the authors report the observer-judged **harmonious : non-harmonious
ratio in the dataset is about 9:1** — severe enough that they applied SMOTE-ENN before
training. **Modern observers call about 90% of real two-piece outfits harmonious.** A harmony
gate applied to real retail garments therefore has a ceiling of roughly 10% discriminating
power before it does anything at all.

**This is the single most important number for the product.** It says colour harmony is close
to a non-constraint on real clothing, and that effort spent refining harmony rules buys
almost nothing. What discriminates must be elsewhere — fit, occasion, register, the wearer.

**Where it breaks.** 20 observers, all ~20 years old (SD 0.94), single cultural context; the
authors name limited generalizability, no racial or cultural diversity, and two-colour
combinations only as their own limitations. Huang's age split at 45 is direct evidence the
effect is age-dependent, so **none of these numbers transfer to an older Ukrainian user
without re-measurement.** The 9:1 ratio in particular may be a property of DeepFashion's
curation — these are catalogue and editorial images, already styled — rather than of clothing.
· **T1 for the correlations and definitions, T2 for the 9:1 prevalence** · default

**New rules:** R-COL-13 (harmony models are uncorrelated on clothing; use as feature, never
verdict), R-COL-14 (tone = L\* × C\*, with the tonal/tone-on-tone/tone-in-tone windows above).

## Oklch+ — verified, and it fixes the axis I measured as broken

Uchida, N. (2026). Oklch+: A Three-Parameter Extension of Oklab for Improved Color Difference
Prediction. arXiv:2606.05255 [eess.IV], 3 June 2026.

**Epistemic status first.** <cite index="30-1">Submitted to Color Research & Application</cite> — **preprint, not peer-reviewed**, single author, independent researcher. The only work citing
it is <cite index="27-1">the same author's own follow-up</cite>. That is a genealogy of one.
**T3 maximum.** It cannot carry `default` in this corpus regardless of how good the numbers
look, and the numbers do look good.

**What it is.** <cite index="24-1">A three-parameter extension of Oklab: a power transformation on the L-axis and a Naka-Rushton compression on the C-axis, with Euclidean distance computed in the transformed coordinates; the Naka-Rushton function is bounded in [0,1], reflecting the saturating nature of chroma sensitivity at high colorimetric values.</cite>

**Results.** <cite index="24-1">On COMBVD — 3,813 suprathreshold pairs across six independent datasets — Oklch+ reaches STRESS 29.09 against CIEDE2000's 29.13, a difference of 0.04, using three fitted parameters against roughly 17 for CIEDE2000. Cross-validation on a held-out BFD-P D65 subset of 2,028 pairs gives STRESS 26.14, with Oklab at 51.45 and CIEDE2000 at 24.12.</cite>

**This independently confirms the defect I measured two audits ago.** Against the Munsell
renotation I found Oklab better than CIELAB on the lightness axis (CV 0.031 vs 0.095) and
**worse on chroma** (0.270 vs 0.199), with badly unbalanced axes (value/chroma 3.14 vs
CIELAB's 1.01). Oklch+ applies a correction to exactly the C-axis, fitted against entirely
different data — colour-difference judgements, not Munsell spacing. Two independent routes,
same diagnosis. That materially raises confidence in the *diagnosis* even while the *fix*
stays T3.

**What it would actually buy us, and it is not accuracy.** STRESS 29.09 vs 29.13 is a tie —
Oklch+ is not more accurate than CIEDE2000, it is equally accurate with three parameters
instead of seventeen. The real gain is structural: **Euclidean distance is a true metric.**
R-COL-07 measured that ΔE00 violates the triangle inequality on **3.0% of real wardrobe
triples**, worst case білий→сірий→чорний off by 18.50. A Euclidean space cannot do that by
construction. **This is what unlocks spatial indexing — k-d trees, ball trees, k-means — over
the Ukrainian catalogue, which ΔE00 structurally forbids.** For a system whose bottleneck is
retrieval over a large feed, that is worth more than a decimal place of accuracy.

**What it would cost, stated plainly.** Oklch+ has **no textile parametric factor.** Adopting
it discards R-COL-11's kL=2 correction, which is the one change in this file with a measured
effect on real garment verdicts. The author's own stated limitation compounds this:
<cite index="24-1">evaluation is limited to the sRGB-centered COMBVD dataset, and validation in high-chroma regions with empirical observer-rated discrimination data remains future work.</cite>
Saturated dyes are exactly the high-chroma region left unvalidated.

**Verdict.** Adopt for **retrieval and clustering** over the catalogue, where metric validity
is the binding constraint and a tie on accuracy is acceptable. Keep **CIEDE2000 at kL=2** for
garment↔garment match verdicts, where the textile correction is measured and Oklch+ has
nothing equivalent. Two metrics for two jobs, each used where its evidence actually reaches.
· **T3 · hint** — preprint, single author, self-citing · **new rule R-COL-15**

**R-COL-01 ⚠ NOTE — Represent colour in a perceptual space (Lab/OKLab), not sRGB/hex.**
All distance, clustering and matching in a perceptual space (sRGB decode → XYZ → Lab, D65).
Hex is storage and display only. · T1 · **hard** · `colorspace.lab`, `colorspace.hx`

**R-COL-02 — ΔE00 for same / different / match decisions.**
Split into two claims with different evidence:
- **The formula** — CIEDE2000 per CIE 142-2001, implementation verified against Sharma,
  Wu & Dalal (2005), *Color Res Appl* 30(1):21–30. · **T1 · default** ·
  `colorspace.de00`, already verified in code.
- **The thresholds** (≤1 imperceptible / 1–2 close look / 2–3.5 at a glance) — **no primary
  source exists.** This is a repeated heuristic. Measured perceptibility, by domain:

  | domain | perceptibility ΔE00 | acceptability ΔE00 |
  |---|---|---|
  | yarn-dyed fabric, CIEDE2000(2:1:1) | 0.62 | — |
  | skin-coloured elastomer, light | 0.7 | 2.1 |
  | skin-coloured elastomer, dark | 1.2 | 3.1 |
  | ceramic shade tabs, dentists | 2.29 | 2.41 |

  A 3.7× spread, driven by material, texture, background and observer expertise — not by
  the formula. **T3 · hint · "folkloric constant, uncalibrated."** For catalogue ingest use
  the textile figure (0.62 at kL=2), which comes from the right material.

**Where it breaks:** the two halves were never reconciled with R-COL-07, which declares the

**R-COL-03 ⬛ vocabulary — L\*C\*h decomposition is the interpreted triple.**
L\* = value / contrast-of-tone (R-CHEV-03); h = hue relation; C\* = saturation / loudness
= Munsell name. Reason and explain in L\*C\*h; keep a crosswalk to Munsell. · T1/T3 ·

Direction of induction and ΔE00 distance are complementary tools, not the same thing —

by metric, direction by induction law, **desirability by the goal gate (R-CHEV-08).**

**R-COL-05 ⚠ NOTE — Harmony via hue angles, but distance ≠ harmony.**
Analogy (near h) and complementary (~180°) operationalize R-CHEV-09. Close is not

**R-COL-06 ⚠ NOTE — [limit] Image colour ≠ real colour.**
Lab is white-point relative (D65); metamerism; catalogue photo colour is unreliable as an

Niakan, Safi, Younespour & Khoshtarkib, *J Prosthodont* 2025, 34(6):602–608,

### R-COL-11 — [new] Cloth needs kL=2. The pipeline is using kL=1.

**Claim.** For textured surfaces, lightness differences are perceptually discounted. The
textile-industry form of the formula is **CIEDE2000(2:1:1)** — kL=2, kC=1, kH=1. The
reference condition kL=1 assumes flat, uniform, untextured samples.

**Source.** ISO 105-J03 lineage; the convention is stated in the parametric-factor
literature (Wang & Luo et al., optimizing parametric factors, PMC9227931). Corroborated on
108 yarn-dyed fabric pairs, 13 observers, three illuminants, where CIEDE2000(2:1:1)
outperformed the alternatives.

**How strongly — measured on the base wardrobe.** 16 colours, 120 pairs, tone-on-tone gate
at ΔE00 < 6. Mean ΔE00 falls **35.8 → 24.2 (32% compression)**. Three pairs flip:

| pair | kL=1 (current) | kL=2 (textile) |
|---|---|---|
| графіт + чорний | 11.13 — visible mismatch | **5.56 — tone-on-tone** |
| білий + світло-сірий | 11.08 — visible mismatch | **5.81 — tone-on-tone** |
| олива + хакі | 9.10 — visible mismatch | **4.93 — tone-on-tone** |

All three are neutrals separated mainly in L\* — exactly the axis kL touches, and exactly
the pairs the gate exists to judge. The overall flip rate (2.5%) understates the impact.

**Where it breaks.** kL=2 was set for woven textile under controlled viewing. It is wrong
for smooth surfaces — patent leather, glossy vinyl, hardware, high-sheen silk — and wrong
for garment↔skin, since skin is not a textile. **Use kL=2 for garment↔garment only.**

· T2 · default · `colorspace.de00` default arg; call sites `colorspace.py:680`,
`outfit.py:974–1002`, `outfit.py:1133`

### R-COL-12 — [new] Normalize chroma against real surface colours, not the display gamut.

**Claim.** The chroma achievable by a dyed reflecting surface is not the chroma achievable
by an emissive display. `c_max()` currently uses the sRGB gamut boundary.

**Source.** Pointer, M. R. (1980), The Gamut of Real Surface Colours, *Color Res Appl*
5(3):145–155 — maximum chroma over a regular lightness × hue-angle grid from 4089
non-fluorescent samples. 576 nodes, L\* 15–90 step 5, h° 0–350 step 10. This is R-MUN-04
with numbers.

**How strongly.** Across all 576 nodes the *means* agree — Pointer 49.7, sRGB 50.9, median
ratio 1.02 — and the *local values* do not. sRGB is wider at 52.6% of nodes and narrower at
47.4%. Worst overshoot **+68.9 C\***, worst undershoot **−45.0 C\***.

| L\* | h° | real surfaces | sRGB `c_max()` | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | 130 | 35.0 | 103.9 | **+68.9** |
| 85 | 140 | 54.0 | 105.8 | +51.8 |
| 60 | 330 | 63.0 | 110.8 | +47.8 |
| 90 | 90 | 90.0 | 45.0 | **−45.0** |

**Effect on the gate it feeds.** `palette.py:558` checks whether a palette window can be
filled by a real garment before issuing it. At C\*≥24 over 540 nodes: passes on sRGB
**89.4%**, on real surfaces **84.6%**; **phantom windows — passed by the gate, unfillable
by cloth — 46 nodes, 8.5%**, i.e. 9.5% of everything the gate lets through. They cluster at
L\*20, h° 50–120° — dark olive, khaki, deep mustard, heavily stocked in Ukrainian retail:

```
L*20 h 50°: code believes C*max=39, cloth reaches 21
L*20 h 60°: code believes C*max=34, cloth reaches 15
L*20 h 90°: code believes C*max=28, cloth reaches 12
L*20 h120°: code believes C*max=32, cloth reaches 14
```

**Discriminating power, stated honestly.** The gate passes 89.4% of the space. Fixing the
ceiling moves it to 84.6%. **This removes a specific 8.5% failure mode; it does not make
the gate load-bearing.** A filter that passes 85% is barely a filter.

**Where it breaks.** (1) Pointer is under Illuminant C, the pipeline is D65; the comparison
above is not chromatically adapted, so near-threshold nodes carry unquantified error — the
±45–69 outliers are far too large to be illuminant. (2) Pointer 1980 is a **floor**: modern
dyes exceed it in most regions, so 8.5% is an **upper bound** and some flagged nodes are
reachable today.

**Do not change this everywhere.** `outfit._гучність` deliberately uses absolute C\* rather
than `rel_C`, because gamut narrowing at low lightness made нейві (rel_C 0.58) and денім
(0.52) read as highly saturated. That reasoning is correct and Pointer strengthens it. The
fix belongs at the **availability gate** only.

· T1 · default · `colorspace.c_max`; fix at `palette.py:558`

### R-COL-16 — [new] The operative garment axis against a wearer is **CIE b\***, and the wearer's variable is **melanin, not undertone class**.

**Claim.** When observers choose a garment colour to suit a particular person, the dimension
their choices actually move along is the garment's **b\*** (blue–yellow), and the person
variable driving it is **skin lightness / melanin level**, not a warm-or-cool classification
of the person.

**Source.** Perrett, D. I. & Sprengelmeyer, R. (2021), Clothing aesthetics: consistent colour
choices to match fair and tanned skin tones, *i-Perception* 12(6),
doi 10.1177/20416695211053361. Twelve White women's faces, six fair (lower melanin) and six
tanned (higher). Experiment 1 N=96, Experiment 2 N=75; two adjustment tasks (hue × value and
hue × saturation) and two instruction sets ("most suits the skin tone" and an explicitly
aesthetic judgement). **Numbers to carry:** 77% (74/96) chose a **higher b\*** garment for
tanned than for fair faces; 71% (53/75) chose warm hues more often for tanned than fair, and
13% (10/75) showed **no** difference. Two popular hue ranges sit almost opposite each other,
**0–45°** and **180–225°**. The authors state that b\* accounts for the choices as well as or
slightly better than splitting the colour circle into warm and cold halves.

**Corroboration on a different population and a different dependent variable.** Zhang, Q.,
Chen, Z. et al. (2026), The impact of clothing colour on skin tone perception and consumer
preference, *Coloration Technology*, doi 10.1111/cote.12828. Twenty-two plain sweatshirts in
a real viewing scenario: **lower-lightness clothing enhances the appearance of fairer skin**,
and **higher-lightness clothing lowers fairness ratings more for models with darker skin** —
i.e. the clothing effect is an **interaction with the wearer**, not a constant.

**Why this matters here specifically.** Gate B of this project's anchor audit reports the
`тепло_сила` axis as **one-sided on the declared population**: on European skin hue
(54.0–77.8°) the "cold" class is never emitted. This is the first source in the theme that
supplies a **sign** for that axis on that population — and its answer is that on this
population the discriminating variable is not the wearer's warm/cool class at all. It is
the **garment's b\*** keyed to the wearer's **L\* / ITA**.

**Where it breaks.** (1) Single primary source for the b\* claim. (2) White women only, and
the manipulated variable is **tan**, which is a within-person state, not a between-person
undertone. (3) Simulated clothing on a screen, adjusted by the observer — not garments, not
fabric, not on a body. (4) Prolific participants, mostly female, ages 19–36. (5) The task is
"suits" and "aesthetically pleasing", which is **not** "I would wear this". (6) Zhang et al.
measure **fairness**, a culturally specific goal that must not be imported into a Ukrainian
product as a target — only the direction of the physical effect transfers, not the
desirability.

# Layer 2 — Mechanism: induction (R-CHEV)

**R-CHEV-01 ⚠ NOTE — Law of simultaneous contrast.**
Two adjacent colours are perceived as maximally dissimilar, in both hue and tone. Two
components: contrast of tone + contrast of colour. Operates wherever colours are adjacent —
skin/fabric, fabric/fabric. · T1 · Chevreul §8, §16 · **hard**
*Unaffected by everything below. The law is not in question; the direction rule was.*

### R-CHEV-02 — Induction direction. **Revised: the complementary law is superseded.**

**Old claim (retained as a special case).** A colour casts its complement onto its
neighbour: red↔green, orange↔blue, yellow↔violet, indigo↔orange-yellow. Chevreul §6, §17,
§43; Fechner 1840.

**Revision.** The complementary law was derived for **achromatic targets** on chromatic
surrounds. Ekroll & Faul (2012b, *Seeing and Perceiving* 25(2):107–141) proposed the
**direction law**: the target is displaced away from the surround **along the extended line
joining surround to target**, so direction depends on **both** colours. Ratnasingam, S. &
Anderson, B. L. (2017), What predicts the strength of simultaneous color contrast?,
*Journal of Vision* 17(2):13 — 30 observers, three experiments — support the direction law
and a generalized Kirschmann's fourth law, and reject the constant-size and inverse-size
hypotheses.

**Why this matters here.** Skin is not an achromatic target: typical skin sits at C\*≈22,
h°≈57. The two laws therefore disagree about skin. On the base wardrobe the mean divergence
is **49°**, and **8 of 16 garments diverge by more than 45°**. They converge only where the
garment hue happens to lie near the skin↔neutral line — mustard, terracotta, denim, navy.
Those are the garments on which the old rule appeared to work.

**Where it breaks.** All of it measured on a CRT, equiluminant, dark room, 0.8° target in a
6°×2.5° surround. A face beside a coat is large-area, non-equiluminant, textured, under
uncontrolled light, viewed long enough to re-admit the adaptation the method suppressed.
The direction result is robust across three experiments; **transfer to face-and-garment
scale has been measured by nobody, including us.**

· T2 · **default** (was hard) · `palette.вікно_нейтралі_за_температурою`
`hard` is unavailable to either law for chromatic targets until a skin-scale measurement exists.

**R-CHEV-03 ⚠ NOTE — Contrast of tone (the value axis).**
The lighter of two adjacent areas looks lighter, the darker looks darker, independent of
hue. The most important and most overlooked component. Feeds the value axis directly;
fabric↔skin value contrast operates separately from hue. · T1 · Chevreul §8–11, §19 ·
**hard** · *Survives §R-CHEV-02 untouched — a separate mechanism.*

### R-CHEV-04 — White / black / grey. **Revised: hue path removed.**

**Retained.** White raises a neighbour's tone (brightens); black lowers it; luminous
complements enliven black. This explains "black whitens the skin" — it lowers the tone, so
light areas read lighter by contrast — and "white freshens / over-exposes."
· T1 · Chevreul §44–70 · default

**Removed.** A garment at C\*≈0 casts **no hue** onto skin, because induction scales with
surround chroma (R-CHEV-13). The corpus previously recorded "grey shows induction most
strongly." In Chevreul that is about grey as the **target** — a grey patch reveals induction
most visibly because it has no colour of its own to mask the shift. Carried into a garment
context, grey is the **surround**, where it induces least.

**Where it breaks.** Catalogue "black" and "grey" are rarely C\*=0. Measured from
photographs a black coat often returns C\*3–8 with a hue direction, and extraction noise is
the same order (smartphone vs spectrophotometer ΔE00≈7.6, Niakan 2025, already in
`colorspace.py`). So: "near zero and below our measurement floor," not "exactly zero."
Note also that at C\*=0 the hue angle is numerically undefined — both чорний and сірий
return h≈297° as artefact, not signal.

**Consequence.** Black at the face is a **pure value decision** — how far garment L\* sits
from face L\*, nothing else. This simplifies the most common garment in the catalogue.
· `outfit.нейтраль` (K-COL-06), `colorspace.achromatic`

**R-CHEV-05 ⚠ NOTE — Successive contrast (afterimage).**
After fixating a colour the eye sees its complement. Relevant to prolonged or sequential
viewing; small effect on a static outfit. · T1 · Chevreul §79 · hint

**R-CHEV-06 ⚠ NOTE — Colour near the face shifts the skin.**
Drapery or a garment near the face induces a shift in perceived skin tone. This is the
mechanism behind "colour near the face." **The existence of the effect is robust; magnitude
depends on light, distance, area and saturation; direction is given by R-CHEV-02 as
revised, not by the complement.** Entry point for personal colour and try-on self-check.
· T2 · Chevreul Part II §III, pp.249–251 · default · `palette.py` ×6

**The orange case is the interesting failure.** "Orange drains fair skin" is Chevreul's most
repeated practical warning, and under the direction law orange produces essentially **zero
hue induction**, because orange sits almost on the skin's own hue line. But the observed
phenomenon is real and every stylist reports it. The resolution is that for a **same-hue,
higher-chroma** surround the induction is in **chroma, not hue** — skin desaturates beside
a more saturated garment of its own hue. That preserves the observation and relocates the
mechanism. **This is a hypothesis generated by the merge, not a sourced finding — T4, hint,
and the most testable thing in this file.**

Every colour choice near skin is an instrument serving a **goal**: heighten a desired tint,

**R-CHEV-09 ⬛ vocabulary — Two families of harmony: analogy vs contrast.**
Analogy (scale / adjacent hues / shared dominant tint) vs contrast (of scale, of hues, of

vocabulary of palette relations for the composer. · T2 · Chevreul §3 · default

**R-CHEV-10 ⚠ NOTE — Complementary palettes intensify most (and read "loud"). Scope narrowed.**
Complementaries side by side give maximum mutual purity and vividness; analogy is quieter.
A saturation/vividness lever at palette level. Cross-link Veblen R-VEB-08: complementary
pair = loud register, analogy tends quiet. · T1/T2 · Chevreul §38–43 · default

### R-CHEV-13 — [new] Induction magnitude scales with garment chroma. The code omits the term.

**Claim.** Colour induction increases with surround saturation whether or not surround and
target share a hue — Kirschmann's fourth law generalizes to targets of any hue.
**Source:** Ratnasingam & Anderson 2017, Experiment 2 — 9 observers, six chromatic
conditions, surround saturation varied against a fixed achromatic reference.

**How strongly.** Monotonic increase over the tested range. The paper is explicit that its
paired-comparison method gives no insight into the rate of increase, whether it saturates at
higher contrast, or behaviour beyond the equiluminant contrasts tested — which were not
highly saturated. **Direction of the relationship is established; functional form is not.**

**Where it breaks.** Do not extrapolate above the tested saturation range; the curve may
saturate or reverse.

**The gap in code.** `palette.py`: `сила_переваги = round(abs(w) * near_вага, 3)` — `w` is
the **wearer's** undertone, `near_вага` is slot proximity to the face. **The garment's own
chroma does not appear.** A muted sage cardigan (C\*14) and a saturated emerald cardigan
(C\*36) in the same slot currently receive identical induction weight. Under the source the
emerald carries roughly 2.5× the pressure — concretely, the emerald is the one that needs a
neutral layer at the collar; the sage is not.

**Proposed form:** multiply by `min(C*_garment / C*_ref, 1)`. **No source supplies
`C*_ref`.** Any value is folkloric until run against wear-verdicts.
· T2 for the relationship, **T4 · hint for any constant** · `palette.сила_переваги`

### R-CHEV-14 — [new] Induction is non-monotonic in hue difference and **zero at 180°**.

**Claim.** Induction magnitude rises with hue-angle difference, peaks near 60–90°, then
declines, reaching zero for hues separated by 180°.
**Source:** Klauke, S. & Wachtler, T. (2015), "Tilt" in colour space: hue changes induced by
chromatic surrounds, *Journal of Vision* 15(13):17; the same envelope falls out of the
direction law in Ratnasingam & Anderson 2017 (their Fig. 11) — two independent routes to
the same shape.

**This contradicts the corpus directly.** R-CHEV-10 and R-COL-05 treat ~180° as the maximum
interaction. For the **skin↔garment pair** the measured curve makes 180° the **minimum**.

**Consequence for recommendation.** For skin at h°57, the strongest hue interactions are at
h≈132° (green) and h≈342° (pink-red) — фуксія and смарагд are the high-pressure garments.
A garment complementary to the skin is the **weakest** hue interaction available, not the
boldest. Navy is not the dramatic choice the old model implied.

**Where it breaks.** The peak location (60–90°) is single-source, from an equiluminant
cone-contrast space in which saturation of the match was fixed and observers could not
report saturation shifts at all. The **shape** is corroborated twice; the **peak angle** is
not. **Do not encode 60–90° as a threshold.** Use the continuous envelope.
· T2 for the shape, T3 for the peak · default · feeds R-CHEV-07

### R-CHEV-18 — [new] Induction **flips sign** at the boundary of the face.

**Claim.** The direction of colour induction on facial skin depends on **where the inducer
sits**. Outside the face — garment, background, hair — induction is **contrastive**, and
R-CHEV-14's envelope applies. Inside the face — lips, eyeshadow, blush — induction is
**assimilative**, and the envelope has the **wrong sign**.

**Source, inside-the-face side.** Kobayashi, Y., Matsushita, S. & Morikawa, K. (2017),
Effects of lip color on perceived lightness of human facial skin, *i-Perception* 8(4),
2041669517717500. Redder lips **lighten** the perceived complexion; darker lips **darken**
it. The authors name two ways this differs from classical lightness contrast: the effect is
assimilative rather than contrastive, and the **inducing area (lips) is far smaller than the
influenced area (skin)** — the reverse of the usual size relation. Experiment 2 attributes it
to **holistic face processing**; the effect is disrupted by inversion. Independent
replication of the direction: Kiritani, Y. et al. (2017), Color illusion on complexion by
lipsticks and its impression, *Jpn J Psychonomic Science* 36(1) 4–16 — orange lips make skin
look yellower, red lips redder and lighter (paired comparisons on Shiseido digital faces, so
the **absolute magnitude there is unknown**, as Kobayashi notes).

**Source, outside-the-face side.** Batres, C. & Russell, R. (2026), Perceived skin evenness
is influenced by background color and hair color, *Perception*,
doi 10.1177/03010066261474995. A high-contrast oval around an **untouched** face makes the
skin read as more even than a low-contrast one; darkening hair does the same. Contrast, not
assimilation.

**This bounds R-CHEV-14, it does not contradict it.** R-CHEV-14 is a **contrast** envelope
and was derived for the skin↔garment pair. A garment is an outside inducer, so the envelope
holds. Extending the same envelope to an intra-face inducer would invert the sign of the
prediction.

**Consequence for recommendation.** In this system the envelope must only ever be fed the
hue of a **candidate garment** against skin, never the hue of a facial feature against skin.
Verified in code: `palette.тиск_знахідка` always receives the candidate garment's hue, even
when the arc that produced the candidate was derived from a makeup colour.

**Where it breaks.** (1) The magnitude is weak: after the 2019 corrigendum
(doi 10.1177/2041669519865375) Experiment 2 gives F(3,57)=3.04, p=.036, ηp²=.14 — this is a
direction, not a lever. (2) Japanese observers and stimuli on the assimilation side. (3) The
boundary itself is a **category, not a measured continuum**: nobody has measured where hair
sits. Hair is adjacent to the face but outside the face oval, and Batres & Russell place it
on the contrast side by result, not by theory. A fringe or a face-framing layer is the
untested case.
· T2 direction / T3 magnitude · hint · consumer: `colorspace.огинаюча_індукції` (recorded as
a boundary; no separate emitter, because the boundary's job is to prevent a wrong extension,
not to fire)

# Layer 3 — Vocabulary and gamut (R-MUN)

Munsell's role here is **naming**, not measuring. It gives a canonical way to say
"deep muted teal" instead of "L\*45 C\*20 h190" — which is what a person understands. This
is its main role for the pilot.

**R-MUN-01 ⬛ vocabulary — hue / value / chroma as three independent perceptual axes (vocabulary).**
Independent and perceptually ordered; a human-nameable layer over Lab. The canonical
dictionary names a colour as (hue family, value, chroma), with a crosswalk to Lab
(R-COL-03). · T1 · default

**R-MUN-02 ⬛ vocabulary — Value is a first-class axis (≈ L\* ≈ contrast-of-tone).**
Roughly the cube root of luminance ≈ perceived lightness. Reinforces R-CHEV-03 and R-COL-03;
structures outfit readability; personal-colour "light/deep" is the value of hair, skin and
eyes. Keep separate from hue. · T1 · default

**R-MUN-03 ⬛ vocabulary — Chroma (muted ↔ clear) is a first-class axis, independent of value.**
"Muted/soft" (low) vs "clear/bright" (high) — palette and personal-colour vocabulary.
Cross-link R-CHEV-10: saturation = loud/quiet. · T1 · default

**R-MUN-04 ⚠ NOTE — The solid is asymmetric: max chroma varies with hue and value.** *(upgraded)*
Yellows peak in chroma at high value; blues and reds at lower value. Limits are those of
vision and pigment together. Do not treat colour as a symmetric wheel; "equally saturated"
depends on hue and value; value–chroma trade-offs constrain which garment colours are
reachable and stocked.

**Now carries numbers** — see **R-COL-12** for Pointer's 576-node table, the 8.5% phantom-
window measurement, and the caveat that Pointer is a floor rather than a boundary. The rule
moves T1 · hint → **T1 · default** because it is now executable rather than descriptive.

**R-MUN-05 ⚠ NOTE — Munsell's axes are the personal-colour backbone; the prescriptions are not.**
Seasonal analysis leans on value (light↔deep), chroma (muted↔clear) and hue-temperature
(warm↔cool) — a real vocabulary, and a principled one. **The mapping person→season is a
separate unvalidated superstructure (R-CHEV-12): Munsell validates the AXES, not the
prescriptions.** · T2/T3 · default

**R-MUN-06 ⬛ vocabulary — [limit] Munsell is vocabulary and ordering, not a distance metric.**
Steps are roughly even within an axis, but axes are not commensurable; Munsell hue ≠ Lab h°;
conversions are non-linear. For "how different / does it match" use CIEDE2000. Never compute
distance in Munsell steps. · T1 · default

**R-MUN-07 ⚠ NOTE — [limit] Surface colours under reference illumination; screen ≠ chips.**
Munsell names describe a reflecting surface under daylight, not screen RGB. Same
illuminant/display caveat as R-COL-06. · T1 · default

### R-PC-03 — Contrast level beats seasonal buckets. *(rebuilt from primary source)*

Previously this rule said only that attractiveness tracks contrast rather than a hue
bucket, sourced to practitioners (Nicholson, Lewis-Crum, Flusser), with no formula, no
magnitude and no failure condition — while being **cited 9× in the code, more than any
other rule in the colour layer.** Rebuilt below.

**Source.** Russell, R., Kramer, S. S. & Jones, A. L. (2017), Facial Contrast Declines with
Age but Remains Sexually Dimorphic Throughout Adulthood, *Adaptive Human Behavior and
Physiology*, DOI 10.1007/s40750-017-0068-x. 151 faces from the FACES database (Ebner et al.
2010), three age bands, identical studio lighting and exposure. Builds on Russell 2009
(*Perception* 38:1211–1219), Porcheron, Mauger & Russell 2013 (*PLoS ONE* 8(3):e57985),
Jones, Russell & Ward 2015 (*Evolutionary Psychology* 13(1):210–229).

**The formula, which the rule never carried.** Facial contrast is Michelson contrast between
a feature and the annulus of skin around it, computed **per CIELAB channel, per feature**:

```
CF = (skin − feature) / (skin + feature)        range −1 … +1
```

Features: eyebrows, eyes (including the band of skin around the lashes), lips. Each against
its own surrounding annulus — **not** against a global skin average.

**Measured values (L\* channel, collapsed across age):**

| feature | female | male | difference | Cohen's d |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eyes | .145 (SE .005) | .118 (SE .004) | .027 | 0.72 |
| lips | .095 (SE .003) | .081 (SE .003) | .014 | 0.53 |
| brows | .117 (SE .006) | .139 (SE .006) | **.022, reversed** | 0.39 |

**Three findings that break the rule as it was written.**

1. **Facial contrast is not one number.** It is at least nine (3 features × 3 channels), and
   they do not move together. Brow contrast runs **opposite** in sign to eyes and lips.
   Collapsing to a single "high contrast / low contrast" scalar destroys the structure that
   carries the signal.
2. **The channels carry different information.** Sex differences are **confined to
   luminance** — there are no sex differences in a\* or b\* contrast (Jones et al. 2015).
   Age declines are **not** confined to luminance: a\* and b\* contrast around eyes and lips
   fall with age (eyes a\* r=−.32 female / −.29 male; lips |a\*| r=−.41 / −.47; eyes b\*
   r=−.41 / −.36). So **L\* is where sex lives, a\*/b\* is where age lives.** A pipeline
   that averages channels reads a mixture of the two.
3. **This is not the variable the industry means.** Practitioner "contrast level" is
   normally **hair against skin**. Russell measures **features against their surrounding
   skin**. Hair is not in the dataset at all. The measured variable and the prescribed
   variable are different variables, and the measured one is the only one with numbers
   behind it. **The empirical support R-PC-03 has been claiming does not cover the hair↔skin
   reading the code uses it for.**

**Where it breaks.** Caucasian and East Asian faces only; age differences studied in
Caucasian faces only. Neutral expression, studio lighting, no cosmetics. Cosmetics move
these values substantially and in the same direction as the sex difference (Jones et al.
2015), so any selfie with makeup reports a shifted value. Between-person SD (.037–.056)
is **larger than every group difference in the table**, so these values describe
populations, not individuals — a single person's contrast cannot be read off the group means.

**Disclosure worth recording:** the lead author reports research funding from a cosmetics
company. The measurements are from a public face database and are independently replicated
across three papers, so this does not undermine them, but it is on the record.

· T2 · default · `palette.py` ×9 · **the rule's scalar reading must be replaced by the
per-feature, per-channel form, or its citation to this evidence base withdrawn.**

### R-PC-10 — [new] Facial contrast survives exposure error but not regional noise.

**Why this exists.** R-PC-04 says extraction from photographs is "noisy." That is true and
useless — it does not say which noise matters. Michelson contrast is a **ratio**, so the two
kinds of camera error behave completely differently, and the difference decides whether the
feature is buildable at all.

**Measured by simulation** on the Russell values (eye contrast, skin L\*66, feature L\*49.3,
20 000 draws):

| error mode | SD of induced CF error | as % of between-person SD (.039) |
|---|---|---|
| common exposure/white-balance shift ±10% (both regions together) | **0.0000** | **0%** |
| independent noise 2 L\* per region | 0.0246 | 63% |
| independent noise 4 L\* per region | 0.0496 | **127%** |
| realistic selfie: ±10% exposure + 4 L\* regional noise | 0.0505 | **130%** |

**Two consequences, both actionable.**

1. **Absolute colour calibration is not required for this feature.** A common multiplicative
   gain cancels exactly in the ratio. This is a genuine relaxation of R-COL-09 for the
   contrast path specifically — normalizing the illuminant helps the *hue* extraction, but
   contributes nothing to contrast measurement.
2. **Directional light across the face is fatal.** Anything that lights the eye region
   differently from the cheek — window light, single overhead source, hair shadow —
   produces exactly the independent regional error that does not cancel, and at 4 L\* it
   already exceeds the entire between-person spread. **The frame gate must reject
   directional lighting, not correct it.**

**Sensitivity, stated directly.** ∂CF/∂L\* ≈ 0.0075 for all three features. The whole sex
difference is consumed by **3.6 L\* units** of skin error for eyes, **2.9** for brows,
**1.9** for lips.

**Where it breaks.** The simulation assumes Gaussian independent noise; real camera error is
structured (JPEG blocking, denoising, skin-smoothing filters) and skin-smoothing in
particular attacks the feature/skin boundary the measurement depends on. The numbers are
therefore an **optimistic floor**. Cross-link R-PC-04 — this quantifies one part of it and
does not replace the hard gate. · T3 (simulation over T2 measurements) · default

### R-PC-04 — Do not auto-diagnose colouring from images. *(numbers added)*

Skin-tone extraction from photographs is noisy, light-dependent, and **worse for darker
skin**. From a selfie → low confidence; take the goal by self-report; emit no confident
verdict; equity risk is explicit. · T1 · **hard** · `colorspace.py`, `palette.py` ×15 —
*the most heavily enforced rule in the colour layer.*

**Now quantified.** Paravina et al., colour-difference thresholds of maxillofacial skin
replications (PubMed 19432759) — 45 observers, 30 skin-coloured silicone pairs, controlled
viewing booth, 50:50% thresholds. In ΔE00: perceptibility **0.7 light / 1.2 dark**;
acceptability **2.1 light / 3.1 dark**. Dark specimens need **~1.7× the perceptibility and
~1.5× the acceptability threshold**.

**Why this strengthens rather than softens the rule.** The gap survives a spectrophotometer
and a viewing booth. It is therefore **not only a camera artefact**, and a single scalar
ΔE00 tolerance applied across all skin tones is systematically stricter on dark skin.

**Where it breaks.** Silicone elastomers, not skin — no translucency gradient, no subsurface
scattering, no sebum specularity, no hair. And this is prosthetics literature, where
matching a prosthesis to a face is a far stricter task than judging whether a garment
flatters. **Import the ratio, not the absolute numbers.** → R-PC-09.

**R-PC-05 ⚠ NOTE — Colour → social perception is real, but SEPARATE from "flatters the skin."**
Red↔attractiveness, dark↔authority are real effects, but they are impression-signalling, not
skin match. Do not conflate the two features (occasion/impression vs colouring harmony).
Cross-link Veblen and person-perception. · T2 · default

**R-PC-06 ⚠ NOTE — [meta] Empirically confirms R-CHEV-12.**
Mechanism real, seasonal taxonomy conventional. Closes the theme's "mechanism vs convention"
question: personal colour = induction goal + contrast match + optional low-evidence seasonal
lens, never a pushed verdict. · T2 · default · `palette.py` ×3

**R-PC-07 ⚠ NOTE — ML confirms seasonal typing is weakly recoverable (~55% on 4-class).**
FaRL64 0.554; Lumina three-region LAB features 0.556 — a heavy transformer extracting a thin
signal. The ceiling reflects both model limits and inconsistency in expert labels.
Quantitatively confirms R-PC-01/04: no confident auto-season; the target itself is
inconsistent. · T1 · default

**R-PC-08 ⚠ NOTE — [productization] A recommender built on 4-season inherits its weak validity.**
Su 2024 pattern (4-season + biometrics → recommender). If a seasonal lens is offered, mark
it low-evidence (R-PC-01); do not build the recommendation core on it; user-invoked lens
only. · T2 · hint

### R-PC-09 — [new] Skin colour tolerance must scale with skin lightness.

**Claim.** A fixed ΔE00 tolerance for "same skin colour" is systematically stricter for dark
skin than for light, by a factor of roughly 1.5–1.7 (R-PC-04 numbers).

**Decision it changes.** `colorspace.same_colour` tolerance (line 680) is currently scalar.
Before: two readings of a dark-skinned user's face are more likely to be called "different"
than two readings of a light-skinned user's, purely from the tolerance. After: the tolerance
scales with skin L\*, and the confidence flag reflects it.

**Where it breaks.** The 1.5–1.7 ratio is from silicone under controlled viewing. Under
phone-camera conditions the real ratio is likely **larger**, since extraction error also
grows with darker skin — so this correction is a floor, not a full fix. It does not remove
the need for R-PC-04's hard gate; it removes one quantified part of the inequity.
· T2 · default

# Layer 6 — Practitioner layer: makeup (K-MUA / R-MUA)

**Why this layer exists, and why its evidence is of a different kind.** Item 7 of "What was
NOT done" in the previous pass read: *no practitioner layer, and this is now the sharpest
gap.* This layer is the first repayment of that debt, and it arrived through a mistake worth
recording. A source search first concluded that the makeup↔clothing question had "no
evidence" because no peer-reviewed study measures it. That conclusion applied the wrong
filter. The correct filter is **named practitioner making a real choice with a stated
mechanism** against **anonymous SEO copy** — not measured against unmeasured. Rule 5 exists
precisely to stop the first mistake.

**The state of the measured literature, stated so nobody re-searches it.** There is a
measured layer for **makeup → face** (R-CHEV-18 above) and a measured layer for
**clothing → face** (R-COL-16 above). There is **no** study that varies makeup colour and
clothing colour together and measures the outcome. That gap will not close by reading more
papers, so any makeup↔clothing rule here is practitioner-sourced by necessity, carries **T3
at best**, and must be given the force an unmeasured rule deserves.

**Source genealogy — count this before counting citations.** The facial-contrast body
(Russell, Porcheron, Morizot, Latreille, Jones, Batres) is largely **one lab plus one
cosmetics company**: Porcheron, Latreille and Morizot are Chanel Recherche et Technologie;
Batres and Russell disclose CERIES funding (a Chanel subsidiary); Batres et al. 2025 was
funded by Chanel Fragrance & Beauty. The work is public-database and independently
replicated internally, so this does not void it — but by this project's own rule it counts as
**one voice, not five**. Genuinely independent lines on this topic: Osaka
(Kobayashi/Morikawa), Chiba/Shiseido (Kiritani — also industry), St Andrews (Perrett), and
the Chinese and Taiwanese colour-science groups (Zhang; Wu).

### K-MUA-01 — [new] Makeup must not repeat the colour of a **large** garment.

**Claim.** A garment occupying a large field must not be the same colour as the wearer's
makeup. Adjacent shades of the same hue remain legitimate; it is **colour identity** that is
excluded, not the hue band.

**Source — practitioners, converging, with the mechanism named.**
- Nathan Johnson, celebrity makeup artist and executive instructor, QC Makeup Academy:
  makeup should **complement** the clothing, not copy it; the working criterion he gives is
  "suits your skin tone **and** is not the exact same colour as what you are wearing"; and
  makeup should tie a look together rather than take it over. **He names the failure and the
  mechanism:** matching a lipstick to a dress goes over the top, and it is hard *because the
  garment is large* — matching a makeup colour to a large garment is the difficult case.
- Runway backstage practice as an industry norm: makeup colours complement the collection;
  makeup **enhances** the clothing rather than competing with it.
- Ukrainian makeup artists, same structure applied inside the face: with a red lip the mouth
  must remain the **only** bright accent; eyes are supported with neutral grey-brown shadow
  and a very fine line so they are neither lost nor turned into a second accent.

**Consequence for recommendation — the before/after.** Without this rule the retrieval layer
returns, under a makeup-derived hue arc, garments of **the same colour as the lipstick** —
and those score **best**, because they sit at the centre of the arc. The rule switches off
exactly the candidates the filter would otherwise rank first. Measured on a rose lipstick
sitting inside the slot window (L\*60.1 C\*28.2 h35.7): the exact colour goes from *passes* to
*rejected* (ΔE00 0.0), while a step darker (ΔE00 7.2), a step lighter (6.7) and a more muted
shade of the same hue (6.3) all continue to pass.

**Why an anti-rule and not an axis.** An exclusion needs weaker evidence than a preference.
"Do not do exactly the same thing" is what practitioners agree on. "Wear this hue with that
lipstick" is what they disagree about and what nobody has measured. Force follows evidence:
this earns an exclusion, not a ranking term.

**Where it breaks.** (1) **Deliberate monochrome** — a full one-colour look with a lipstick
to match is a legitimate device, and this rule cuts it. It must be overridable from the
intent layer above, **not** by moving the threshold. (2) The ΔE00 ≤ 5.0 threshold is a **T4
folklore constant**: practitioners say "the same colour", not "ΔE00 < 5". Calibration action:
show judges garment+lipstick pairs at graded ΔE00 and find where "the same colour" stops
being read. Until then the number carries no authority. (3) "Large" is implemented as a slot
list (верх, низ, сукня, верхній_шар), not as a measured area fraction; the area term exists
in `areas.py` and this rule does not yet use it.
· T3 source / T4 threshold · **anti-rule (exclusion)** · consumers: `palette._рядки_від_рис`
emits `не_ближче_за`; `feed.підходить` enforces the excluded ball · registered in
`outfit.ПОЗА_КОРПУСОМ` until this entry is merged into `корпус-знань.md`

### R-MUA-02 — Makeup and clothing come from **one** palette, not two.

**Claim.** Makeup is not a separate colour problem coordinated afterwards with the clothes.
It is drawn from the **same person-derived palette** as the garments, which is why
practitioners in this tradition need no lipstick↔dress rule at all: coherence is inherited.

**Source.** Christine Scaman, 12 Blueprints (Sci\ART lineage, Kathryn Kalisz), states the
mission of the practice as helping a client select and combine her palette colours in **every
item of apparel she wears, including makeup and hair colour**. The analysis runs on three
continuous axes — hue (warm/cool), value (light/dark), chroma (clear/muted) — read from
calibrated drapes, not from a season label.

**Consequence for architecture.** This is the justification for makeup entering the profile
as **features with their own hue arcs from the same window generator** rather than as a
separate makeup module. It also predicts that a second, parallel "makeup palette" component
would be redundant — a specific failure it prevents.

**Where it breaks.** The seasonal *taxonomy* attached to this lineage is rejected elsewhere in
this corpus (R-PC-01, R-PC-08) and nothing here revives it. What is taken is the **three-axis
reading and the single-palette principle**, not the twelve buckets. Inter-analyst reliability
for draping has never been published, by anyone, in any system.
· T3 · principle, not an emitter · consumer: architecture of `colorspace.додати_макіяж` +
`palette.тон_від_рис`

### R-MUA-03 — A lipstick is chosen on **two axes at once**: undertone harmony **and** contrast against the skin.

**Claim.** The practitioner criterion is not "match the undertone". It is harmony of undertone
**together with** a deliberate lightness/chroma contrast against the complexion. Either axis
alone gives a known failure.

**Source.** Lisa Eldridge: the most flattering red is one that harmonises with the undertone
**while creating contrast** against the skin; a blue-toned red brightens and often makes teeth
read whiter; a warm brick or orange red adds warmth and sits particularly well on golden and
olive complexions. **Conditions with named failure modes:** baby pinks carry a lot of white
pigment, so they belong on very fair cool skin — on medium or olive skin the same shade stops
being neutral and reads as a deliberate 1960s **statement**, i.e. it jumps register rather
than failing outright. For olive complexions the recommendation is **muted, desaturated
shades**, because a bright carnation pink gives a bolder result than intended. Eldridge also
states plainly that for pinks there are **no hard rules** — recorded here because a
practitioner marking her own limit is evidence about force.

**Consequence.** This is the practitioner form of the two-axis split the code already has:
hue arc (harmony) and contrast lever (K-CLR-03). It confirms the shape and supplies the
conditions the code lacked.

**Where it breaks.** These are shade recommendations for a person, and this product **does not
advise makeup** — it reads makeup as an input. So R-MUA-03 has **no executable consumer
today**, and is recorded as research-layer knowledge, not as a live rule. The widely repeated
wrist-vein test that accompanies this advice in the popular layer is **not** carried: it has
no reliability data behind it.
· T3 · research layer, no emitter

### R-MUA-04 — The interest budget runs **through the face**, not only through the garments.

**Claim.** The spotlight constraint this corpus already applies at outfit level (§3.5 of
Theme 6, "interest budget: person against garment") has an intra-face twin, and makeup is the
lever on the person's side of that budget. One loud feature; the rest supported, not
amplified.

**Source.** Ukrainian makeup artists on the red lip: the mouth stays the **only** bright
accent, eyes are carried with neutral grey-brown shadow and a very fine lash line — the
stated failure being that red lip plus a strong eye "looks too dramatic in real life", while
under-supporting the eyes loses them. Eldridge's olive/baby-pink condition is the same
budget seen from the other side: the shade does not fail, it **spends more of the budget than
intended**.

**Consequence, and it is not yet built.** If the wearer's own makeup already spends the loud
slot, the garment loudness budget should shrink accordingly. The system now ingests makeup
colours as named features, so the input exists; the coupling does not.

**Where it breaks.** No magnitude anywhere. "Loud" is not defined on the face in any source
found, and the obvious candidate — chroma × area — fails immediately, because R-CHEV-18 shows
the lips influence a field far larger than they occupy. Area weighting on the face is an open
problem, not a missing line of code.
· T3 · research layer · named implementation path: join to the existing loudness budget once
face-area weighting has a defensible form

## 6.0 Measurement: the person axis was inert on 11,695 real SKUs

**What was run.** `measure_person_axis.py` on the Ager women's sub-feed (14,030 SKUs; garment colour from the `Цвет` param through `verify.ЛЕКСИКОН`; 11,695 SKUs resolve to a lexicon window). Four opposite colourings (warm-light, cool-dark, cool-light, warm-dark), four schemes, the live path `специфікація → запит_у_фід → feed.підходить`. Because a colour word is a *window* (40–60 L\* wide, 60+ C\* wide), two bounds were computed.

| bound | what passes | Jaccard between opposite persons |
|---|---|---|
| pessimistic (window centre as a point) | 6,045 neutrals for everyone; chromatic garments through hue windows: **0 of 5,650** | 0.93–1.00; scheme "tonal": **1.00** on all six pairs |
| optimistic (window ∩ window ≠ ∅) | 95–100 % of the catalogue for everyone | **1.00** |

Per-axis rejection for warm-light, tonal, slot "верх" (L 48–76, C 14–34, h 160–184°): L rejects 3,213, C 837, h 1,600 of 5,650. The chroma ceiling of 34 rejects everything the lexicon calls "синій" (centre C 46), "червоний" (65), "зелений" (52). Only the lightness windows on `uncontrolled` produced 8–22 % difference between persons. `тиск_тону`, `тон_від_рис`, the neutral b\* window: all computed, none changing any SKU.

**Reading.** The domain map's "person and garment colour work on a real catalogue" described a pipe that runs, not a decision that is made. The cause is not the rules: the name-window branch added on 2026-08-23 passed the *centre* of the lexicon window as a point, and `підходить` asked a point "yes/no". The corpus's own principle — "the person is a zone in value space, not a label" — applies equally to a garment known by a word.

**Fix (code, 2026-08-24).** `feed.частка_вікна(window, query)`: the fraction of a 4×4×6 grid over the name window (L×C×h) that passes the *same* `підходить` that judges photo points. `прогін` carries `вікно` and `колір_ім` into the catalogue; `покриття` admits any garment with fraction > 0 and ranks by fraction (photo points first with fraction 1.0); `виправданий_вибір` reports `частка_вікна` and `джерело_кольору`.

**After.** Chromatic candidates before → after: warm-light 0 → 2,429, cool-dark 0 → 1,507 (neutral+accent); Jaccard of top-300 between opposite persons 0.00–0.13 instead of 1.00; warm-light's top-300 is powder pink 208 + light blue 92, cool-dark's is blue 300.

**Where it breaks, named.** (1) Mean fraction 0.03–0.09: the number says "this much of what 'синій' can be would fit" — honest uncertainty, not precision. (2) Every SKU of the same colour word has the same fraction: the choice among 300 "синій" must come from cut, price, formality; `відрив_від_другого` is zero by construction. (3) The grid quantises: an overlap thinner than ~¼ of the window reads as 0. (4) Warm-dark gets zero chromatic candidates under tonal and analogous — either the chroma band 26–34 is unreachable by catalogue names or her garments sit among the 3,009 SKUs (26 %) without a slot from the name; to check separately. (5) The fraction assumes the true colour is uniform over the name window — unmeasured. (6) This is not a substitute for measuring garment colour from photos (`колір_з_фото`); it is the honest path until that measurement exists. Force of any number: `hint`.

**Gate.** Add `measure_person_axis.py` to the regression with the condition "Jaccard of opposite colourings < 0.9 on at least one scheme"; otherwise the axis goes silent unnoticed.

### R-COL-16 — revised: the wearer's variable is eye colour, not skin melanin

**Was.** "The operational garment-side axis against the wearer is b\*; the wearer's variable is melanin, not undertone class" (Perrett & Sprengelmeyer 2021). Named next step: `b_min/b_max` keyed on ITA.

**Now.** The same laboratory tested *which* feature carries the effect: Perrett, D. I. (2023; print 2025). *Eye color is more important than skin color for clothing color aesthetics.* Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts 19(5):1099–1110. doi 10.1037/aca0000626. Experiment 1 (100 participants): faces of White women, skin transformed between low and high melanin with hair and eyes unchanged — **clothing colour choices did not change**. Experiment 2 (100 new participants): eyes transplanted between light-eyed and dark-eyed faces — choices **followed the eyes**: light eyes → cooler, lighter, less saturated clothing (light blue); dark eyes → warmer, darker, more saturated (brick red / orange). Participants instructed to "match the skin" still matched the eyes. Perrett's own wording: a "remarkably high" level of agreement; "surprisingly, skin colour has little influence"; and the condition he states himself: at conversational distance (lunch, interview) the eyes matter; at runway distance skin may matter.

**Consequence for 2021.** The six tanned and six fair faces of 2021 were different women whose skin melanin, hair and iris darkness covaried; 2023 unconfounds this in favour of the eyes. So `b_min/b_max` keyed on ITA is the **wrong key**; the key is iris lightness/warmth. The 2021 record stands as a measurement of the garment-side direction (b\*); the wearer's variable is replaced. See R-COL-17.

### ПРОВЕНАНС_РОЗВИЛКИ["контраст_образу∝контраст_особи"] — not single-lineage

**Was.** "Near-unanimous but ONE lineage — seasonal PCA (Color Me Beautiful)".

**Now.** The same move is made independently by: Flusser, *Dressing the Man* (2002) — the tailoring tradition, not PCA: dark hair against light skin is a "contrast complexion", similar hair and skin a "muted complexion", and "the trick is to repeat the degree of contrast in the clothes that frame the face"; Lamport (Inside Out Style) — image consultant, measures contrast on a grey scale; JPCA (NPO 日本パーソナルカラー協会) — sells "attribute-scale drapes" (属性スケールドレープ) to test one axis at a time, lightness among them; WARM N COOL (Seoul) — colorimeter plus drapes. **Cross-lineage on the move; the variable differs** (K-CON-01): Flusser hair↔skin, Lamport the spread of three features including eyes, the code hair↔skin. Agreement on the principle is not agreement on the measurement.

### R-CHEV-16 (inter-reflection) — a practitioner-observable sign appeared

Scaman (12 Blueprints, "Sharing a Colour Journey"): reflection of the drape's colour on the skin (the chin) is read as a sign of **mismatch** — "reflection is usually the body's way of saying 'this isn't me'"; exception: some Summers, where reflection flatters. Practitioners therefore *see* the R-CHEV-16 mechanism and give it a sign: visible reflection = too far. Magnitude still absent; sign T3.

### K-CLR-02, "over" branch — conflict with the only measurement, recorded, unresolved

K-CLR-02 penalises a near-face lightness jump larger than the person's own contrast (`over` = 15, T3). The only measurement of facial surround lightness is Batres & Russell 2026 (*Perception*; already R-CHEV-18): **higher** contrast of the face's surround (hair, clothing, background) with the skin makes the skin read **more even**; the authors suggest the known advantage of black clothing in attractiveness ratings (over red, blue, green, yellow, white in prior work) is partly explained by this. The direction is opposite to the "over" branch. Not a refutation (evenness ≠ "the clothes wear the person"), but **the threshold of 15 has nothing to stand on** and the measurement pulls upward. `strong` for the "over" branch exceeds verification; hold `default` until measured on own verdicts.

### R-COL-17 — [new] Clothing colour is chosen to the eyes, not to the skin

**Claim.** Observers consistently choose clothing colour by **iris** colour; skin has little influence. Light eyes → cooler / lighter / less saturated; dark eyes → warmer / darker / more saturated. Source: Perrett 2023 (6.1). 200 participants across two experiments.

**Where it breaks.** (1) One laboratory, one population; (2) hair was not transplanted separately — "little influence" of hair holds only in Exp. 1 where it did not vary; (3) screen, not fabric; (4) the general preference for red/blue overlays the effect.

### R-CHEV-19 — [new] Kirschmann's third law: hue induction is attenuated by lightness contrast

**Claim.** Simultaneous colour contrast is maximal when there is no lightness contrast between centre and surround, and is strongly reduced as that contrast grows (Kirschmann 1891; repeatedly confirmed; modern review and the thin-line exception: Scientific Reports 2020, doi 10.1038/s41598-020-77241-5). Kaneko et al. 2018: induction is stronger for brief presentation (10–40 ms) than for prolonged (80–640 ms) — prolonged viewing weakens it, which supports R-CHEV-17 and the practitioners' "you can't see it in a photo, you can in the room".

**When it applies.** Skin↔garment pairs where the garment has hue (C\* above threshold). Reliable for areas; exception — thin contours (piping, trim), where the law does not hold.

**How strongly.** The form of the decay is **uncalibrated** — no source gives a constant for faces. Any function is T4.

**Where it breaks.** Measured on patches, not faces.
· **T1 law / T4 form** · `default` for the direction · `colorspace.тиск_тону(…, L_речі, L_шкіри)`; `REGISTRY["_КІРШМАН_ШИРИНА"]` = 40, T4; default `None` keeps old behaviour · **Decision (measured, warm-light, skin L\* 75.8):** before, "синій" (L\*≈37) and "блакитний" (L\*≈72) of one hue got the same multiplier — none. With the illustrative monotone decay 1/(1+|ΔL\*|/40): зелений 1.00→0.61, м'ятний 0.96→0.90 — **mint overtakes green** as the loudest hue beside this face; червоний 0.81→0.44, рожевий 0.89→0.85. Ranking changes; magnitudes remain uncalibrated.

### K-ECHO-01 — [new] "Repeat your own colours" — cross-lineage agreement with a measurement on the eyes

**Claim.** Three independent practices derive garment colours from the person's features.
- Flusser (2002): "repetition of one or more of the face's tones in the surrounding apparel"; features in order of importance: skin, hair, eyes, special pigmentation (freckles, blush); "a dark face surrounded by a white shirt yields considerable contrast" — the garment against skin creates contrast regardless of hair.
- Zyla (*Color Your Style*, 2010), from theatrical costume: eight colours. Romantic (blush/cheek flush, inner lip), Dramatic (the darkest ring of the iris — "your black", the formal dark neutral), Energy (the lightest part of the iris), Tranquil (the mid iris / vein colour in the cool reading), Essence (the lightest skin tone — "your white"), First Base (iris ring → darkest formal neutral), Second Base (hair colour → "your brown"), Third Base (lighter hair / skin-hair blend → "your khaki", casual neutral). Zyla's own caveat: colours are "evocative", rarely literal; readers' caveat: vein/palm colours do not read on dark skin.
- Lamport: "signature colours" from individual colouring ("dark brown will flatter me as that's my hair colour"); "the more a print/pattern/texture resembles you naturally, the better"; triads that replicate the person's own scheme (blue eyes + red lipstick).
Measurement on the eye component: Perrett 2023 (R-COL-17); Radeloff 1990 — observers agree with practitioners' recommended colours (validates the practice, not the theory).

**When it applies.** The practitioners **disagree on the order** (Flusser: skin first; Perrett: eyes first). Resolved in favour of the measurement (rule 5 in reverse: a measurement arbitrates between practitioners), with the distance condition of R-COL-17.

**Where it breaks.** Dyed hair (Zyla gives alternative loci); grey hair — achromatic, votes only by lightness (K-TIME-01); veins/palms unreadable on dark skin; Zyla "rarely literal".

### K-PAL-01 — [new] The practitioners' rule for generating a palette from a person

**Claim (the rule, not a table).** Sci\ART (Kalisz, *Sci\ART Personal Color Analysis Training Manual*, 2003; lineage Chromology, True Colour International, 12 Blueprints): "the millions of colours we see are assigned into 12 harmony groups. Each tonal group is attributed with different predominant colour characteristics, which are defined by their hue, value and chroma"; a person has a **dominant characteristic** on one of the three axes (light/deep, warm/cool, bright/soft) and a **secondary** on another; the palette is the region of colour space that **shares** these characteristics; "not all harmony groups contain all the colours of the rainbow, but all 12 tones get their versions of primary hues such as blues, greens, yellows and reds"; "a single colour can only be assigned to one harmony group"; the tones "were not developed by simple selection from the Munsell tree" (TCI); "individual clients often have their own personal favourite neutrals and accents within their palette, in effect creating their own 'microclimate' within their tone" (TCI). Lamport: 18 palettes from the same three criteria ("how warm or cool you are; whether you're lighter or darker; how bright or soft you are"). JPCA: four attributes, single-axis drapes. Practitioner caveat (InfinitCloset 2023): "particularly difficult for darker skin tones (they would all be Dark at first glance) … are you Dark for somebody of your ethnicity, or just compared with a pale Caucasian?"

**Validation of the practice.** Radeloff 1990 (already R-PC-02): 276 observers agree with the practitioners' recommended colours in 5 of 6 sets. **What is taken.** The generation rule: axis positions → a window that shares the dominant characteristic → every hue family in the person's version (warm red = tomato/brick/coral vs cool red = raspberry/berry; warm blue = teal/turquoise/petrol vs cool blue = cobalt/periwinkle/navy — conventional practitioner colour vocabulary, T3) → the microclimate from features (K-ECHO-01) → the eye family always present ("your own colours are in your palette"; Zyla; Perrett 2023).
**What is not taken.** Seasonal labels as a mechanism (R-PC-01) and any table of the form "Winter wears black".

## 6.3 Density defect found and fixed (balayage)

`colorspace.density` looked for peaks only in interior bins; two dense clusters at the ends of the L range (roots L\* 18, ends L\* 55) sat in the first and last bins and were **always** merged into one averaged mode L\*≈33 — present in no strand — reproduced with 8 and 200 samples and with transition tones. Consequence: wrong person contrast, wrong "her black". Fix: edge bins count as peaks; the valley test still decides bimodality. After: 8 samples → (17.1, 57.9); single-colour hair does not split. Limits measured: three tones → three sub-features; four tones ~20 L\* apart merge to two averaged modes (smoothing + valley 0.65, T3); a 5 % highlight is an inclusion, not a voice; a streak at the same lightness but different hue is invisible (density is one-dimensional along the dominant axis); a smooth ombré has no modes and yields one averaged mode — the spread (q90−q10) is kept in `F["_розподіли"]`, and `personal_palette.смуга_світлоти` now takes the ends of that spread, not the middle. The honest description of multi-coloured hair is: darkest end, lightest end, temperature of the bulk, plus any chromatic strand (a permanent near-face accent, K-COL-02) — not a count of modes.

## 6.6 Kettlewell / House of Colour lineage — combining within the palette, "wow" colours, re-rating

Kettlewell (UK's largest online colour retailer, 20+ years, 300+ shades coded by season and tone, House of Colour lineage; colour-coding "by well trained and experienced eyes, under expensive and often revealing full spectrum lighting. But eyes, nonetheless"):

- **Wow colours = the microclimate.** "Knowing your seasonal sub-type means knowing which colours from your wider palette will work best for you … all of the colours within your wider seasonal palette will also work for you, and will harmonise with your sub-type 'wow' colours." The wide palette is the region; the sub-type is the narrower best part "for your own skin tone and contrast level". Same structure as K-PAL-01 + microclimate.
- **Combining inside the palette (K-PAL-15 support).** "With versions of every colour within every palette, it should be possible to find a complementary combination for your seasonal palette. The same guidelines apply to each of the seasonal palettes — just choose 'your' version of each of the shades"; start with two colours, "whether they are analogous, complementary or tonal"; "the shades next to your face will flatter your skintone" (K-PAL-09).
- **Light-dominant placement rule (K-PAL-09 refinement).** "To achieve harmony and balance, you need to use light to medium depth colours. Do not overpower your natural colouring with dark combinations and when you wear the darker colours of your palette, keep the lighter shades near your face." Deep/clear types: "you will almost certainly look your best with a good degree of contrast within your outfit — mix light and dark tones".
- **Context changes a colour's palette membership — K-PAL-16 [new].** "If we wear a colour in a high contrast way, it looks far brighter than if it is paired with soft muted shades. Likewise, a neutral colour worn with warmer shades looks more like it belongs to the warmer palette, and worn with cooler shades …". This is the garment↔garment simultaneous-contrast mechanism (R-CHEV) stated as a practitioner device: a boundary colour can be **pulled into** the palette by what it is paired with. Condition: boundary colours only; force `hint`; code: not implemented — the device would modify `частка_вікна` of a boundary garment by its companions' temperature/chroma.
- **Re-rate with age (K-TIME-01 support).** "We also recommend visiting your consultant for a 'Colour Re-rate' every 5–10 years as your star rating within your palette is likely to change as you age."
- **Boundary honesty.** "As we come closer to the boundary line between warm and cool, or muted and bright, it becomes ever trickier for the naked eye to distinguish"; some colours are coded to two palettes. Supports continuous positions over labels.

## 6.7 Nachmias (12 Blueprints / Sci\ART) — K-ECHO-01 is contested inside the practice

Rachel Nachmias (Best Dressed, Philadelphia; 12 Blueprints certified, Sci\ART method; head-to-toe styling and wardrobe curation):

- **Against feature echo as a rule.** "'This goes with my hair' … will be obviously wrong to the many cool or cool neutral people who have brown eyes and hair. Wearing most browns and colors with a warm, earthy undertone brings out blotchiness and flaws which are not present in the correct (cooler) colors. Other brown eyed brunettes, however, wear these colors with no detriment to their skintone. Why is that? They have apparently similar overtones and completely different undertones." In Sci\ART draping the **hair is covered**: the verdict is read from the skin's reaction, not from the hair's overtone. "Modern color analysis involves observing the simultaneous contrast effects on the face caused by colors with different qualities and neutralizing them to reveal the most flawless, youthful and healthy skin appearance."
- **Resolution in code (K-ECHO-01 caveat).** Hair-derived neutrals keep their b\* window only when the skin's side (palette axis `hue`, from a reliable photo or self-report) does not oppose it; otherwise the row goes out without the b\* window and is marked `оскаржено = K-ECHO-01/Nachmias`. Eye-derived accents are unaffected (Perrett 2023 measured observers' choices; Nachmias's objection is about skin reaction to hair-matched browns). The corpus now holds both: feature echo as a practitioner device with a measured eye component, and the Sci\ART objection that overtone ≠ undertone.
- **Value-axis observables for K-PCA-01.** "The appearance of overly severe bone structure is often linked to shadowing, often caused by overly dark colors. Conversely, the appearance of puffiness can often be caused by lack of defining shadows, which many times happens when a color is too light." "A Light season may notice on her own she looks strict and angry in black." Added to the K-PCA-01 table: too dark → harsh shadows / severe; too light → puffy / undefined. Her own caveat: "without a specific comparison done in a controlled setting, as we do in a PCA, it's hard to be sure which is the true face."
- **What a full service includes** (the stylist deliverable list the system is measured against): draping verdict; hair colour and makeup shade recommendations with products; "how to correctly identify your colors at home and while shopping"; review of the client's own wardrobe and makeup against the result; a physical palette fan; wardrobe gap analysis and shopping options by budget. Of these the system now gives: the palette with its own combinations, the at-home identification (names, hex, windows), and the wardrobe review only as a future function (theme 7); it deliberately does not give hair-colour or makeup-shade advice (R-MUA-03).

## 6.10 JPCA official position (NPO 日本パーソナルカラー協会, p-color.jp) — the verdict as an effect, not a match

Original wording, with translation:
- 「当協会のパーソナルカラーでは、色の四属性(色相・明度・彩度・清濁)の違いによる顔の見え方が客観的な法則性をもって変化するというパーソナルカラーにおける「色の効果」を世界で初めて明快にしました」— "Our association was the first to make explicit the 'effect of colour' in personal colour: the appearance of the face changes with objective regularity according to differences in the four attributes of colour (hue, lightness, saturation, clear/muddy)." → the four-attribute frame is the association's founding claim (R-MUN-08 confirmed at the source).
- 「「色の効果」は誰もが共通に感じることができるので、「似合う理由」をわかりやすく説明ができます」— "Because everyone perceives the 'effect of colour' in common, the reason a colour suits can be explained clearly." 「当協会公式調査：約90％が同様の見え方をすると回答」— "association survey: about 90 % report seeing the same appearance." → an inter-observer agreement claim on the *effect*, self-reported survey without published method: T3; it does not touch the corpus statement that inter-**analyst** agreement on verdicts is unpublished.
- 「実は、肌の色と似合う色が同じとは限りません。時には、不健康に見えてしまったり、肌の悩みが目立ってしまったりなど、逆効果になってしまう場合もあるのです」— "In fact, the colour that suits is not necessarily the same as the skin's colour. Sometimes it can backfire: making one look unhealthy, or making skin concerns stand out." → independent support for Nachmias (6.7): similarity to the person's own colours is not the criterion; the observables are the K-PCA-01 ones (unhealthy look, skin concerns visible).
- 「パーソナルカラーとは、その人の肌、瞳、唇などの色に調和する色（似合う色のグループ）のことです。とはいえ、肌の色に似た色とは限りません」— "Personal colour is the group of colours that harmonise with the person's skin, eyes, lips … though not necessarily colours resembling the skin." → the referents are skin, eyes and lips (lips = a given feature — K-MUA's "given vs chosen" line runs through natural lip colour).
- 「例えば「お顔を色白に見せたいなら、この色が効果的です」など、具体的に説明ができる」— "For example: 'if you want your face to look fairer, this colour is effective'." → JPCA states the verdict as an **effect per goal**, which is exactly the two-goal frame of R-CHEV-08 (heighten / dissimulate) and the objective form of K-PCA-01; note the cultural target ("fairer") that does not transfer (Zhang 2026 caveat).

**Consequence for the corpus.** K-ECHO-01 now has two practitioner lineages against literal echo (Sci\ART via Nachmias; JPCA) and three for it (Flusser, Zyla, Lamport), with the measurement (Perrett 2023) on the eyes only. The code's resolution — echo the eyes; derive neutrals from hair only when the skin's side agrees; state verdicts as effects — sits inside this disagreement rather than pretending it away.

## 6.12 Closed 04.09.2026 (code) — the accent the code prescribed is checked by the code

- **K-PAL-18** the accent role is verified, not merely assigned: `pipeline.акцент_образу(речі, спец_слоти, кандидати, за_ід)`, wired into `перевірити_від_моделі` and reported as `K-PAL-18` findings.
  **WHEN it applies:** a single scheme was chosen (branch known) and the specification gave at least one slot the role «акцент» with a chroma floor (`C[0]`, from `palette.СХЕМИ[..].хрома["акцент"]`); the item in that slot has a measured colour (`lab`, or `hex` → Lab). Not in the «не обирала» mode (four schemes, roles differ, no single prescription), and not for items whose colour was never measured — silence there is honest, a verdict would not be.
  **HOW STRONG:** two findings, deliberately different in force and address. (a) The pool for that slot contained items at or above the chroma floor and the outfit still carries none: `soft`, side «код» — a choice against the code's own specification; addressed to the prompt and the model's selection. (b) The pool contained no such item: `hint`, side «дані» — a catalogue deficit; addressed to sourcing, and blaming the model here is an attribution error that would trigger the wrong repair. The chroma floor is the scheme's own number, so this check inherits its provenance and adds no constant of its own.
  **WHERE it breaks:** chroma is computed from the catalogue record (`lab`), never from the pool record sent to the model (which carries no `lab`); on the first run the pool record was used, every item read as unmeasured, and every failure was booked to the catalogue — a standing accusation instead of a measurement. An aggregate finding («the whole scheme collapsed to neutrals», `слот=None`) was written and deleted the same run: the graph attaches findings to items, so it never reached the person. Real case that produced the rule: red chosen, scheme «нейтрали+акцент», accent on shoes and bag, both hands returned an all-black outfit (C ≈ 6 against a floor of 28) — the judge issued eight findings and none about colour; measured afterwards, each pool had 22 items above the floor.
